But God Meant It Unto Good

His brothers sold him, a liar framed him, and a prison forgot him. Years later Joseph looked at the very people who did it and spoke the sentence that heals families: ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good.

Word and Faith Network · Genesis 50:19-21
"And Joseph said unto them, Fear not: for am I in the place of God? But as for you, ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good, to bring to pass, as it is this day, to save much people alive. Now therefore fear ye not: I will nourish you, and your little ones. And he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them."
Genesis 50:19-21, KJV

Some of the deepest wounds you carry did not come from strangers. They came from your own house. A brother who turned on you. A friend who sold you out. A family that talked about you instead of to you. A person you trusted with your heart who handed it to someone else for a price. And you have been carrying it for years — the memory, the replay, the question you still can't answer: how could they? If that's you, I want you to meet a man who was hurt by his own blood, buried by his own brothers, and lied on by people he served faithfully. And I want you to hear the sentence he spoke at the end, because it might be the sentence that sets you free today.

The Pit Had a Price Tag

Joseph was seventeen. He had a dream, a coat of many colours, and a father who loved him openly. That was enough for his brothers to hate him. "And they could not speak peaceably unto him." Then one day, far from home, they saw him coming and said, "Behold, this dreamer cometh." They stripped off his coat. They threw him into a pit with no water. And then — this is the part that cuts — they sat down to eat bread. They ate lunch while their brother cried in a hole.

Then they sold him. Twenty pieces of silver. They dipped his coat in goat's blood and let their own father believe a wild beast had torn him apart. Hear me: Joseph's pain was not imaginary. It was not a misunderstanding. It was not something he needed to "just get over." It was real evil, done on purpose, by people who should have protected him. And God never once asks you to pretend that what happened to you didn't happen.

Ye Thought Evil

And it got worse before it got better. Joseph served Potiphar with excellence, and Potiphar's wife lied on him. He went to prison for a sin he refused to commit. In prison he helped the king's butler, and asked one thing: "Think on me." And the Bible says, "Yet did not the chief butler remember Joseph, but forgat him." Two more full years. Betrayed by family. Framed by a boss's wife. Forgotten by the man he helped. Some of you know every one of those chapters.

So when Joseph finally stands before his brothers in Genesis 50, notice he does not lie about it. "Ye thought evil against me." He names it. Forgiveness is not calling evil good. Forgiveness is not saying "it was nothing." Joseph looked them in the eye and called it exactly what it was. But he didn't stop there — and that is where everything changes.

"He sent a man before them, even Joseph, who was sold for a servant: whose feet they hurt with fetters: he was laid in iron: until the time that his word came: the word of the LORD tried him."Psalm 105:17-19, KJV

But God

"Ye thought evil against me; but God meant it unto good." Two words, and the whole story turns. They meant the pit to be the end. God meant it to be the road. They meant the silver to sell him. God used it to send him. They meant the prison to bury him. God used the prison to put him one conversation away from Pharaoh. The same events — two completely different authors. One wrote evil. The other overwrote it.

Don't miss this: God did not author their sin. He doesn't need your enemies' cruelty. But He is so sovereign, so wise, so far above it all, that He can take the very thing they did to destroy you and make it carry you into your purpose. "To save much people alive." Joseph's pain became a nation's bread. Here's my question for you today: what if the thing that broke you is the very place God is about to feed other people from? Before he ever saw his brothers again, Joseph named his sons. One name said, God made me forget my toil. The other said, God made me fruitful right here — in the land of my affliction.

"And the name of the second called he Ephraim: For God hath caused me to be fruitful in the land of my affliction."Genesis 41:52, KJV

Am I in the Place of God?

Now look at the moment. Jacob is dead. The brothers panic. "Joseph will peradventure hate us, and will certainly requite us all the evil which we did unto him." For seventeen years Joseph has fed them in Egypt, and they still think he's just been waiting for Daddy to die so he can get them back. They fall down before him: "Behold, we be thy servants." This is it. Joseph has the power, the position, the guards, the throne. One word and it's over.

And Joseph weeps. Then he says, "Fear not: for am I in the place of God?" In other words: judgment is not my job. Payback is not my assignment. I am not going to sit on a throne that belongs to God alone. Some of you have been trying to sit in that seat for years — replaying the wrong, rehearsing the speech, waiting for the day they finally get what they deserve. Get up out of God's chair. It was never built for you. And it is crushing you.

I Will Nourish You

Then comes the most beautiful line in the whole chapter. "Now therefore fear ye not: I will nourish you, and your little ones. And he comforted them, and spake kindly unto them." The brothers who threw him in a pit — he feeds them. The men who sold him — he provides for their children. The ones who spoke against him — he speaks kindly to them. That is not weakness. That is the strongest man in Egypt choosing grace. That is what it looks like when a wounded man becomes a healed man.

So today, I'm asking you to do what Joseph did. Name it honestly: they meant it for evil. Then lift it higher: but God meant it unto good. Get out of the place of God and give Him back the judgment seat. And then — this is the hard part — let God make you a source of bread instead of bitterness. Say it out loud: "What was meant to destroy me, God is turning for my good. I will not stay in the pit, I will not sit in God's seat, and I will not let bitterness feed on my future." The pit was real. The prison was real. But it was never the last chapter. God has the pen. And He meant it unto good. In Jesus' name.
