Come Boldly to the Throne of Grace

You were never meant to approach God with fear — you were invited to come with boldness.

Word and Faith Network · Hebrews 4
"Let us therefore come boldly unto the throne of grace, that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need."
Hebrews 4:16 — KJV

There is a word in this verse that stops many believers before they ever get to the throne: boldly. Not timidly. Not cautiously, wondering if today is a good day to ask. Not hoping God is in a merciful mood. Boldly — with confidence, with freedom of speech, with the settled assurance of a child who knows the door is already open.

This is remarkable, because the throne being described is not an ordinary one. It is the throne of the Most High God — holy, set apart, the seat of absolute authority over heaven and earth. And yet the writer of Hebrews does not tell us to approach it with dread. He tells us to come boldly. Something has changed the terms of access.

What Changed the Terms

The verse does not stand alone — it is the conclusion of an argument. The verse just before it says: "Seeing then that we have a great high priest, that is passed into the heavens, Jesus the Son of God, let us hold fast our profession" (v.14). Our boldness is not based on our own standing. It is based on who is seated at the right hand of the Father, interceding for us.

"For we have not an high priest which cannot be touched with the feeling of our infirmities; but was in all points tempted like as we are, yet without sin."Hebrews 4:15, KJV

Jesus is not a distant judge who has never known your struggle. He was tempted, tested, wearied, grieved — and remained without sin. He is both fully able to sympathize with your weakness and fully qualified to intercede for you without fault. That is the foundation under the word "boldly." You are not storming the throne on your own merit; you are walking in on His.

A Throne of Grace, Not Judgment

Notice also what kind of throne this is. Scripture elsewhere speaks of a great white throne of judgment (Revelation 20:11) — but that is not the throne Hebrews invites us to. This is "the throne of grace." For the believer in Christ, the throne we approach is not one that keeps score. It is the throne from which mercy and help flow freely to those who come.

This does not mean sin no longer matters, or that reverence is unnecessary. It means the posture of approach has changed. You are not coming to beg a reluctant God to notice you. You are coming to a Father who has already made a way, who is already disposed toward mercy, and who is simply waiting for you to come.

Mercy and Grace, Precisely Timed

The verse ends with a promise built for the exact moment you need it: "that we may obtain mercy, and find grace to help in time of need." Not grace in general, stored up for someday — grace to help in time of need. Whatever you are facing today, this is not a throne that operates on a delay. Mercy for what you've done wrong. Grace for what you cannot do on your own. Both are available the moment you come.

"My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness."2 Corinthians 12:9, KJV

God's grace does not wait for you to be strong enough to deserve it. It meets you precisely in the weakness, the need, the place where you have run out — because that is exactly where grace is designed to work.

The Word for Today

Whatever has kept you at a distance from God today — shame, fear, the sense that you need to clean yourself up before you pray — lay it down. The invitation stands: come boldly. Not because you have earned a place at the throne, but because the One who sits there has already made a way, and He is not waiting to turn you away. He is waiting for you to come.
