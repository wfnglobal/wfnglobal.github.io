Go Again Seven Times

Six times the servant looked toward the sea and came back with the same report: there is nothing. Elijah never argued with the report — he just said, go again. The seventh time, a cloud like a man's hand, and the prophet told the king to get his chariot ready.

Word and Faith Network · 1 Kings 18:43-44
"And said to his servant, Go up now, look toward the sea. And he went up, and looked, and said, There is nothing. And he said, Go again seven times. And it came to pass at the seventh time, that he said, Behold, there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man's hand. And he said, Go up, say unto Ahab, Prepare thy chariot, and get thee down, that the rain stop thee not."
1 Kings 18:43-44, KJV

Three and a half years without a drop of rain. The brooks dried up. The fields cracked open. The cattle died in the stalls. And on the top of Mount Carmel, one man had just watched fire fall out of an empty sky and lick up the water in the trench. The whole nation fell on its face. Everybody else went down the mountain to celebrate. Elijah went up the mountain to pray. Because the fire had fallen — but the rain still hadn't.

Fire Is Not the Finish

Before he ever prayed, Elijah told Ahab, "there is a sound of abundance of rain." Look at the sky. Not a cloud. Not a breeze. He heard it before he saw it. That is how faith works: the word arrives first, and the evidence shows up later. God had already said, "I will send rain upon the earth." The promise was settled in heaven. But the promise still had to be prayed down to earth.

So Elijah climbs to the top of Carmel, casts himself down upon the earth, and puts his face between his knees. That is not the posture of a man performing. That is the posture of a woman in labour. He is not asking God whether it will rain. He is pressing until it does. Some things God has promised you will not fall into your lap because you agreed with them once on a Sunday. They come when you get low, get serious, and stay there.

"Elias was a man subject to like passions as we are, and he prayed earnestly that it might not rain: and it rained not on the earth by the space of three years and six months. And he prayed again, and the heaven gave rain, and the earth brought forth her fruit."James 5:17-18, KJV

"There Is Nothing"

"Go up now, look toward the sea." The servant climbs, scans the horizon, and comes back with the most discouraging report in the Bible: "There is nothing." Not "a little." Not "it's coming." Nothing. Blue sky. Dry wind. Same as yesterday. Same as three years ago.

I know that report. You prayed for your son and he still isn't calling. You believed for the job and the inbox is still empty. You stood on the healing scripture and the scan came back the same. You looked toward the sea, and there is nothing. And this is exactly where most people stop praying — not because God said no, but because the horizon said nothing. Hear me: "nothing" is a report about the sky. It is not a verdict on the promise.

Go Again

Watch Elijah. He doesn't rebuke the servant. He doesn't explain the delay. He doesn't call a meeting to work out what went wrong. He says three words: "Go again seven times." Go again. Then again. Then again. Six trips up the hill. Six times the same answer. Nothing. Nothing. Nothing. Nothing. Nothing. Nothing.

And what is Elijah doing while the servant keeps coming back empty-handed? He is still on his knees. The report never moved him, because his faith was never in the report. Jesus said it plainly: "men ought always to pray, and not to faint." Fainting is just quitting on trip number six. Some of you are one trip away. You have been faithful through six nothings. Do not let the sixth one talk you out of the seventh.

"And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."Galatians 6:9, KJV

A Cloud Like a Man's Hand

"And it came to pass at the seventh time, that he said, Behold, there ariseth a little cloud out of the sea, like a man's hand." A little cloud. The size of a fist. Most people would have been disappointed. Three and a half years of drought, six trips up the hill, and all you've got is a cloud the size of a hand? But Elijah didn't measure the cloud. He recognized it. It looked like a man's hand — because it was the hand of God, rising out of the sea to answer His servant.

Don't despise the small beginning. The first phone call back. The first good report. The first night you slept without fear. The first payment that went through. Heaven very often answers with a fist before it answers with a flood. If you're waiting to see the whole storm before you believe, you will walk right past the cloud that's carrying it.

Get Your Chariot Ready

Then comes the boldest line in the passage: "Go up, say unto Ahab, Prepare thy chariot, and get thee down, that the rain stop thee not." One hand-sized cloud, and Elijah is already issuing a weather warning to the king. And in the meanwhile "the heaven was black with clouds and wind, and there was a great rain." The man who saw the fist knew the flood was on its way.

So here's my question for you today: what have you stopped sending your servant up the hill for? What did you used to pray about every single morning that you quietly gave up on after the sixth nothing? Go again. Pray again. Look toward the sea again. And the moment you see the smallest sign that God is moving, don't wait for the downpour to start thanking Him — prepare your chariot. Get ready for what you prayed for. The God who answered by fire is the same God who answers with rain, and He has not forgotten one word He spoke over you. Go again. In Jesus' name.
