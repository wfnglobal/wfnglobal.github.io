He Shall Doubtless Come Again

In a lean year the seed in the farmer's hand was his children's supper, so he walked into the field crying — and planted it anyway. Heaven wrote one word over his harvest that no drought can cancel: doubtless.

Word and Faith Network · Psalm 126:5-6
"They that sow in tears shall reap in joy. He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him."
Psalm 126:5-6, KJV

There is a kind of crying nobody sees. It doesn't happen at the funeral. It happens on a Tuesday, in the car, in the two minutes before you walk back into the thing you promised God you wouldn't quit. You're still showing up to the marriage. Still giving out of a thin account. Still praying for the child who stopped praying years ago. You're doing the right thing — with wet eyes. And a voice keeps asking, "What is the point?" If that's you this Sunday, I want to take you to an old song the pilgrims sang on the road up to Jerusalem. It ends with a word heaven only uses when it means it: doubtless.

Like Them That Dream

Psalm 126 is a Song of Degrees — a road song, sung uphill. It opens with a memory: "When the LORD turned again the captivity of Zion, we were like them that dream. Then was our mouth filled with laughter, and our tongue with singing." They had come home from Babylon. It was so good it felt fake. Even the heathen nations said it out loud: "The LORD hath done great things for them."

Then the song turns. Verse four: "Turn again our captivity, O LORD, as the streams in the south." Wait — weren't they already free? Yes. And they still needed God again. That's honest. You can have a testimony behind you and a famine in front of you on the same day. Yesterday's miracle does not cancel today's need. And the streams in the south were the dry riverbeds of the Negev — cracked, empty, dead all summer, until rain hit the hills and the wadi ran full overnight. That's the prayer. Lord, do it again. Do it suddenly.

Weeping, Bearing Precious Seed

Then the psalm paints a farmer. "He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed." Why is the seed precious? Because it's scarce. In a lean year, the bag of grain on his shoulder is the same bag that could feed his children tonight. Every handful he throws in the dirt is a handful nobody eats. So he cries. Not because he has no faith. Because obedience costs.

And notice what God does not say. He doesn't say, "He that stops crying, and then sows." He says he goeth forth and weepeth. The tears and the seed leave the house together. Heaven never asked you to feel fine before you obey. Some of the greatest seed ever sown went into the ground from the hands of people who were sobbing.

"Thou tellest my wanderings: put thou my tears into thy bottle: are they not in thy book?"Psalm 56:8, KJV

Buried Is Not Lost

David said God keeps your tears in a bottle. Sit with that. The God who numbers the stars keeps an inventory of your crying. The night over that prodigal — on record. The hospital parking lot — bottled. You thought those tears evaporated. They were collected.

And here is the hard thing about seed: it has to disappear before it multiplies. Put it in the ground, and for weeks it looks like you lost it. Nothing above the soil. Your field looks exactly like the field of the man who planted nothing. That's where most people quit — in the in-between, after the sowing and before the showing. But buried is not the same as gone. Jesus said it plainly: "Except a corn of wheat fall into the ground and die, it abideth alone: but if it die, it bringeth forth much fruit." Buried seed is not lost seed. It's working seed.

Doubtless

Now the word. "Shall doubtless come again with rejoicing." Not possibly. Not hopefully. Not if the economy cooperates. Doubtless. The Holy Ghost took every doubt out of that sentence before you ever got to read it. Your feelings are allowed to have questions. The harvest doesn't.

And look what he comes back carrying: "bringing his sheaves with him." He went out with seed. He came back with sheaves. He went out with a handful. He came back with an armful. He went out weeping. He came back rejoicing. God does not return what you sowed. He returns it multiplied. Your tears were the down payment — never the final statement.

"And let us not be weary in well doing: for in due season we shall reap, if we faint not."Galatians 6:9, KJV

There's a clock in that verse: due season. Not your deadline. Not your birthday. The moment the harvest is actually ripe. And Paul gives one condition, only one: if we faint not. The harvest is guaranteed. Quitting is the only thing that can forfeit it.

Go Forth Anyway

Remember who sowed in tears first. In Gethsemane, Jesus prayed "with strong crying and tears." Then He went out bearing the most precious seed heaven owned — Himself — "for the joy that was set before him." Three days in the ground. Then He came again with rejoicing, bringing His sheaves with Him. And if you belong to Him, you are one of the sheaves. You are proof that tears planted in God's will come back as harvest.

So here's my question for you today: what are you crying over that God told you to keep planting? The marriage. The ministry with twelve people. The business with three customers. The child. The obedience nobody claps for. Don't eat your seed because you're hungry. Don't stay in the house because you're sad. Go forth. Weep if you must. But sow.

Then say it out loud before this day gets away from you: "They that sow in tears shall reap in joy." And say this one too: "I shall doubtless come again with rejoicing." Wipe your face. Pick up the bag. Your tears are watering something you cannot see yet. Weeping may endure for a night — but joy cometh in the morning, and it's coming back with sheaves. In Jesus' name.
