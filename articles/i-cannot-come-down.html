I Cannot Come Down

The wall was half built when the invitations started — a friendly meeting in the plain of Ono, four times, same offer. Nehemiah saw the trap behind the courtesy and gave the same answer every time: I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down.

Word and Faith Network · Nehemiah 6:2-4
"That Sanballat and Geshem sent unto me, saying, Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono. But they thought to do me mischief. And I sent messengers unto them, saying, I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down: why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you? Yet they sent unto me four times after this sort; and I answered them after the same manner."
Nehemiah 6:2-4, KJV

Some of you are building something right now. Nobody sees it yet. A sober life, one day at a time. A marriage you are rebuilding brick by brick. A business at half-height. A prayer life you finally started taking seriously. And you've noticed something strange: the closer you get to finishing, the more invitations you get to stop. A call. An argument. An old friend. A sudden crisis that has to be handled right now. If that's you this morning, meet a man who had a wall to finish and a phone that would not stop ringing.

Half the Height, Twice the Noise

Jerusalem's walls had been rubble for generations. Then Nehemiah, a cupbearer to a Persian king, heard the report, wept, prayed, and came home to build. The enemy tried mockery first. Tobiah laughed: "if a fox go up, he shall even break down their stone wall." It didn't work. "So built we the wall; and all the wall was joined together unto the half thereof: for the people had a mind to work."

Then came threats. That didn't work either — they built with one hand and held a weapon with the other. And by chapter six there was "no breach left therein." Only the doors were missing. That is exactly when the enemy changed tactics. Hear me: the attack gets quieter when you are close. The ones who couldn't stop you by force will try to stop you by friendliness.

Come, Let Us Meet Together

"Come, let us meet together in some one of the villages in the plain of Ono." It sounds so reasonable. A meeting. A conversation. Let's talk things out. No swords, no shouting, just an appointment. But the Bible pulls back the curtain in five words: "But they thought to do me mischief."

Not every invitation is an opportunity. Some distractions come dressed as diplomacy. Some traps come with a calendar invite. Ono was miles away from the wall, out on the low plain. Every distraction is like that. It always asks you to leave the high place where you are building and come down to the low ground where you can be handled. The enemy who couldn't stop you by attack will try to stop you by appointment.

I Am Doing a Great Work

Now listen to Nehemiah's answer, because it is the whole sermon: "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down." He didn't say, "I'm busy." He didn't say, "Maybe next week." He named the size of what he was carrying. A man who knows his work is great will not trade it for a meeting in Ono.

Here's the problem with most of us: we don't think our work is great. So we come down for anything. We come down for the argument in the comments. We come down for the old relationship that texts "hey stranger." We come down for the offence, the gossip, the grudge. Your healing is a great work. Your children are a great work. Your purity, your praying, your calling — great work. Stop treating your assignment like it's small enough to interrupt.

"Therefore, my beloved brethren, be ye stedfast, unmoveable, always abounding in the work of the Lord, forasmuch as ye know that your labour is not in vain in the Lord."1 Corinthians 15:58, KJV

Why Should the Work Cease?

Then Nehemiah asks the question every distracted builder must ask: "why should the work cease, whilst I leave it, and come down to you?" Distraction is never free. When you leave the wall, the wall stops. Every hour you spend in Ono is an hour your wall stands half-finished. The enemy doesn't need to destroy what you're building. He just needs you to stop.

And notice: "they sent unto me four times after this sort; and I answered them after the same manner." Same invitation. Same answer. Nehemiah didn't need a fresh revelation every time the phone rang. He needed the same no, said again. Then they tried slander in an open letter. Then they tried fear — "let us meet together in the house of God, within the temple, and let us shut the doors of the temple." And Nehemiah answered, "Should such a man as I flee?" Persistent distraction deserves a persistent answer.

"And Jesus said unto him, No man, having put his hand to the plough, and looking back, is fit for the kingdom of God."Luke 9:62, KJV

So the Wall Was Finished

And then the most beautiful sentence in the book: "So the wall was finished in the twenty and fifth day of the month Elul, in fifty and two days." Fifty-two days. Generations of ruin, finished in fifty-two days by a man who would not come down. And when the enemies heard it, "they were much cast down in their own eyes: for they perceived that this work was wrought of our God." The ones who kept calling you down will be the ones cast down when they see what God finished through you.

So here's my question for you today: what is calling you down right now? Name it. The person. The habit. The screen. The fight you keep getting dragged into. The fear that keeps saying, "run and hide." It has sent for you four times already. It will send again tomorrow. And you don't need a new strategy. You need Nehemiah's sentence and Nehemiah's prayer: "Now therefore, O God, strengthen my hands."

Say it out loud: "I am doing a great work, so that I cannot come down." Then decline the meeting. Silence the notification. Walk away from the plain of Ono. Pick the trowel back up and go back to the wall. You're closer than you think. Stay up. Finish it. In Jesus' name.
