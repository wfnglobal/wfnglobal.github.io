Little by Little, Not All at Once

God never promised Israel an instant Canaan. He promised a mighty hand, a God among them, and a deliverance that would come by little and little — on purpose, not by accident.

Word and Faith Network · Deuteronomy 7:19, 21–23
"The great temptations which thine eyes saw, and the signs, and the wonders, and the mighty hand, and the stretched out arm, whereby the LORD thy God brought thee out: so shall the LORD thy God do unto all the people of whom thou art afraid. Thou shalt not be affrighted at them: for the LORD thy God is among you, a mighty God and terrible. And the LORD thy God will put out those nations before thee by little and little: thou mayest not consume them at once, lest the beasts of the field increase upon thee. But the LORD thy God shall deliver them unto thee, and shall destroy them with a mighty destruction, until they be destroyed."
Deuteronomy 7:19, 21-23, KJV

Moses is standing at the edge of Canaan with a nation that has every reason to be afraid. Walled cities. Nations bigger and mightier than them, by God's own description a few verses earlier. And yet Moses doesn't talk them out of being outnumbered — he talks them into remembering. Before he says one word about strategy, he points backward: to Egypt, to the plagues, to the Red Sea. Because the God who split a sea doesn't need Israel to feel ready for Canaan. He needs them to remember Who already went ahead of them once before.

Remember What Your Own Eyes Saw

"The great temptations which thine eyes saw, and the signs, and the wonders, and the mighty hand, and the stretched out arm, whereby the LORD thy God brought thee out." This is not secondhand testimony. Moses says thine eyes saw it. These were people who had personally watched the Nile turn, who had personally walked through a sea on dry ground with walls of water on either side. And God's instruction through Moses is simple: take what you watched Me do once, and let it tell you what I am both willing and able to do again. "So shall the LORD thy God do unto all the people of whom thou art afraid."

Whatever you're facing today probably isn't your first fight. Somewhere behind you is a testimony — a bill that got paid when you couldn't see how, a door that opened, a diagnosis that didn't go where it was headed, a relationship God healed. That memory isn't nostalgia. It's evidence. The same God who did that is standing in front of what's in front of you now.

Thou Shalt Not Be Affrighted

"Thou shalt not be affrighted at them: for the LORD thy God is among you, a mighty God and terrible." Notice what the command is actually built on. It isn't "don't be afraid because the enemy is weaker than you think." It's "don't be afraid because of who is standing among you." The size of the giants never changes in this chapter. What changes is whose presence Israel is being told to weigh them against.

"If God be for us, who can be against us?"Romans 8:31, KJV

Fear almost always starts with bad math — sizing up the obstacle against your own strength instead of against His. The giants in your life are real. So is the God who is among you, mighty and terrible on your behalf, not just in theory but in history, the same history your own eyes have already watched Him write.

By Little and Little, Not All At Once

Here is where the chapter gets honest in a way a lot of our prayers aren't. God doesn't promise Israel an overnight Canaan. "The LORD thy God will put out those nations before thee by little and little: thou mayest not consume them at once, lest the beasts of the field increase upon thee." A slow deliverance here isn't a weak one — it's a wise one. Too much victory too fast, and the land itself would turn wild before Israel had the numbers to steward it. God paced the battle to match their capacity to carry the blessing.

If the thing you're believing God for hasn't come all at once, that is not proof He forgot you. Some breakthroughs are withheld, in His wisdom, not because He is slow, but because the ground isn't ready for everything the breakthrough would bring yet. He is not just interested in winning the battle in front of you. He's interested in whether you'll still be standing, intact, to actually live in what He wins.

Until They Be Destroyed

But little by little was never meant to sound like forever. "The LORD thy God shall deliver them unto thee, and shall destroy them with a mighty destruction, until they be destroyed." The pace is patient. The outcome is not in question. Every nation listed in this chapter that stood between Israel and the promise had an expiration date on it the moment God spoke this verse — whether it fell in year one or year seven of the conquest.

"Being confident of this very thing, that he which hath begun a good work in you will perform it until the day of Jesus Christ."Philippians 1:6, KJV

Whatever is being pushed out of your life little by little right now — a habit, a fear, a stronghold, an opposition you didn't choose — take Deuteronomy 7 at its word. The timeline is His to set. The outcome was decided before the battle started.

The Word for Today

You have already watched God move once. Let that memory, not your fear, set the terms for what you expect from Him next. He is among you — mighty and terrible on your behalf — and whatever He is removing from your life by little and little, He is removing on purpose, at a pace you can actually carry, toward an ending that was never in doubt. Do not be affrighted. He brought you out before. He will do it again.
