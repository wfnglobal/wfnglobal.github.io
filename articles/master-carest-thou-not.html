Master, Carest Thou Not?

The storm was filling the boat and Jesus was asleep on a pillow. The disciples woke Him with the question every frightened heart has asked — Master, carest thou not that we perish? He answered the sea with three words: Peace, be still.

Word and Faith Network · Mark 4:38-40
"And he was in the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow: and they awake him, and say unto him, Master, carest thou not that we perish? And he arose, and rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still. And the wind ceased, and there was a great calm. And he said unto them, Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?"
Mark 4:38-40, KJV

Some storms don't make the news. They make your stomach drop at 2 a.m. The test result. The layoff email. The call from the school. The bank balance that doesn't add up no matter how many times you refresh it. You prayed. You did what He said. And the water is still coming over the side of the boat. Worst of all, heaven feels quiet — like Jesus is somewhere in the back, asleep. If that's you this Monday, I want to take you to a little fishing boat on the Sea of Galilee, a pillow, a storm, and the most honest question the disciples ever asked: Master, carest thou not that we perish?

Let Us Pass Over

Before the storm, there was a sentence. Mark 4:35: "Let us pass over unto the other side." Jesus said it. Not the disciples. Not a fortune-teller. The Son of God named the destination before they ever untied the boat.

Hear this, because it will save you a lot of confusion: they were not in the storm because they disobeyed. They were in the storm because they obeyed. Jonah got a storm running from God. These men got one following Him. So stop asking, "What did I do wrong?" every time the wind picks up. Sometimes the storm is not proof you missed God. It's proof you're crossing over to something He has already named.

Asleep on a Pillow

Then it hit. "There arose a great storm of wind, and the waves beat into the ship, so that it was now full." These were fishermen. They knew this lake. When professionals panic, it's bad. And where is Jesus? "In the hinder part of the ship, asleep on a pillow." Not pacing. Not bailing. Sleeping.

Why could He sleep? Because the storm was no threat to the One who made the sea. You don't lose sleep over something that has to obey you. His rest was not neglect. It was authority lying down. And here's the comfort: the same Jesus who closed His human eyes in the boat is the God who never closes His eyes over you.

"Behold, he that keepeth Israel shall neither slumber nor sleep."Psalm 121:4, KJV

Carest Thou Not?

So they wake Him — and listen to how they do it. Not "Lord, save us." Not yet. They say, "Master, carest thou not that we perish?" That's not a prayer. That's an accusation wearing a prayer's clothes. The storm didn't just fill their boat. It filled their theology. Because when the water rises and heaven is quiet, the enemy whispers one lie louder than all the others: He doesn't care.

Let me answer that lie for you. He cared enough to get in the boat. He cared enough to come down from glory, wrap Himself in flesh, and sleep in the same storm you're in. He cared enough to climb a cross. Don't you ever measure His love by the noise of the wind. Measure it by the nails.

"Casting all your care upon him; for he careth for you."1 Peter 5:7, KJV

Peter wrote that. Peter was in that boat. The same man who once asked, "Carest thou not?" lived long enough to write the answer: He careth for you. Some of you are in chapter four of Mark. You're going to live to write First Peter five.

Peace, Be Still

"And he arose." Hallelujah — three of the best words in the Bible. He didn't scold the disciples first. He dealt with the storm first. "He rebuked the wind, and said unto the sea, Peace, be still." The word Jesus used there is the word you'd use for muzzling an animal. He talked to the sea like it was a dog barking at His friends. Sit. Hush. Enough.

And Mark keeps score: a great storm, then a great calm. Not a little calm. Not the wind dying down slowly over three hours. "The wind ceased." Immediately. The God who can stretch out a heaven can shut down a hurricane with three words. Whatever is roaring at you today — the debt, the diagnosis, the dread — it is not louder than His voice. It just hasn't heard Him speak yet.

Why Are Ye So Fearful?

Then He turns to them: "Why are ye so fearful? how is it that ye have no faith?" Not "why did you wake Me?" He doesn't mind being woken. Wake Him all you want — that's what prayer is. What grieved Him was that they forgot what He'd already said. Let us pass over unto the other side. Jesus never promised them a calm crossing. He promised them an arrival. And a boat with Jesus in it cannot sink with His word still unfulfilled.

So here's my question for you today: what did God tell you before the storm started? Go back to it. The promise He gave you in the calm still stands in the chaos. The storm can rock the boat. It cannot rewrite the destination.

So wake Him — but wake Him with faith, not accusation. Stop asking, "Do You care?" and start saying, "I know You care. Speak to this." Then say it out loud before the wind gets another word in: "Master, I know You care for me." And this one: "Peace, be still — I'm going over to the other side." The wind is loud. The waves are high. But the One in your boat has never lost a crossing. In Jesus' name.
