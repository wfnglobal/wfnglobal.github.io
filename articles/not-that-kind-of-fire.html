Not That Kind of Fire

James and John wanted lightning for a village that said no. Jesus wanted mercy. What you ask God to burn down says more about your spirit than theirs.

Word and Faith Network · Luke 9:51-55
"And when his disciples James and John saw this, they said, Lord, wilt thou that we command fire to come down from heaven, and consume them, even as Elias did? But he turned, and rebuked them, and said, Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of."
Luke 9:54-55, KJV

Somebody needs to hear this before they open their mouth today: being right about the offense does not make you right about the response. Jesus had just been snubbed — flatly, deliberately, for no better reason than His destination. And the first instinct out of two of His own disciples was not patience. It was petition for fire. Not a quiet grievance. A request for heaven to open up and torch an entire village. And Jesus' answer to that request is the whole sermon: not "no," not an explanation — a diagnosis. Ye know not what manner of spirit ye are of. Somebody needs that sentence today more than they need the fire they're asking for.

He Set His Face Like Flint — And Got Shut Out Anyway

Luke tells us exactly where Jesus' heart was: "He stedfastly set his face to go to Jerusalem." That is not casual travel. That is resolve — the kind that doesn't flinch, because He knew what was waiting for Him there: betrayal, a cross, and resurrection on the other side of it. He sent messengers ahead into a Samaritan village to prepare the way. And the village said no. Not because of anything He did. Not because of anything He said. They rejected Him because of where He was headed — an old grudge between Samaritans and Jews over Jerusalem, centuries deep, and Jesus just happened to be standing in the middle of it.

Let that sit with you. Jesus got rejected for a prejudice that had nothing to do with Him personally. And if it happened to Him, it will happen to you — you will get shut out of rooms, dismissed from conversations, and written off for reasons that are not really about you at all. The question the rest of this sermon answers is: what do you do next?

Fire Worked for Elijah. It Wasn't James and John's Assignment.

James and John didn't invent their request out of thin air. They had precedent — 2 Kings 1, where Elijah called down fire on soldiers sent to arrest him, not once but twice. So in their minds, this wasn't overreaction. This was Bible. This was tradition. This was "we're just doing what the prophet did." And that is exactly the trap: something happening in Scripture does not automatically make it your assignment for today. Elijah's fire answered a specific confrontation in a specific covenant season. James and John were standing next to Mercy Himself, on His way to die for the very kind of people who had just rejected Him — and they wanted to skip straight to judgment.

Here is the warning in that for us: you can quote the right chapter and still be in the wrong spirit. You can have scriptural precedent for your anger and still be completely out of step with what God is actually doing in the room. Before you call down fire on somebody, ask a harder question than "is this justified?" Ask: "is this Mine to call down?"

"But I say unto you, Love your enemies, bless them that curse you, do good to them that hate you, and pray for them which despitefully use you, and persecute you;"Matthew 5:44, KJV

That is not a suggestion for the people who are easy to love. That is the standing order for the ones who just shut the door in your face.

Rejection Is Not Permission

Here is where this gets uncomfortable, because none of us are above it. Somebody cuts you off in a conversation, in a relationship, in a deal, in a church — and something in the flesh starts drafting the fire. Not literal fire. Our version is sharper words, a public callout, a cold shoulder dressed up as "boundaries," a grudge we tell ourselves is righteous indignation. We don't ask God to rain down lightning anymore. We just do it ourselves, one post, one silence, one cutting remark at a time. Same spirit. Different century.

Rejection will always try to hand you a match and call it justice. Jesus is showing you, in real time, what it looks like to hold the match and put it down anyway. He didn't deny the offense — He just refused to let it decide who He was. Being wronged is real. Being wronged is not a commission.

Ye Know Not What Manner of Spirit Ye Are Of

This is the line that should stop every one of us in our tracks. Jesus doesn't correct their theology. He questions their spirit — what is actually driving this request underneath the Bible verse they're quoting to justify it? Zeal can be real and still be the wrong spirit. Passion can be sincere and still be flesh wearing scripture as a disguise. That is why discernment has to go deeper than "can I defend this?" and all the way down to "what is actually fueling this in me right now — love, or wounded pride dressed up as righteousness?"

"Let all bitterness, and wrath, and anger, and clamour, and evil speaking, be put away from you, with all malice: And be ye kind one to another, tenderhearted, forgiving one another, even as God for Christ's sake hath forgiven you."Ephesians 4:31-32, KJV

Next time rejection stings and your mouth starts loading ammunition, stop and ask Jesus' question of yourself before you ask it of anybody else: what manner of spirit am I of right now? That one pause has saved more relationships, more reputations, and more witness for Christ than any clever comeback ever has.

He Came to Save, Not to Scorch

Luke doesn't leave the story hanging on a rebuke. He closes it with the reason behind it: "For the Son of man is not come to destroy men's lives, but to save them." That is the mission statement underneath every refusal Jesus ever absorbed. He was not thin-skinned about rejection because He was not living for approval — He was living for a cross that would save the very village that just turned Him away. And that is the whole gospel in miniature: you and I were once the village that said no — walking in darkness, no welcome mat out for Him at all — and He did not call fire down on us either.

"He has delivered us from the power of darkness and conveyed us into the kingdom of the Son of His love." Not because we earned a welcome. Because Mercy kept walking toward Jerusalem anyway, carrying a cross instead of a lightning bolt, for people who had every reason to expect judgment and got salvation instead.

The Word for Today

Somebody reading this got rejected recently — by a person, a room, a door that should have opened and didn't — and you have been drafting the fire ever since. Put it down. Jesus never told the disciples the village didn't deserve a response. He just refused to let them carry the wrong spirit into it. Luke tells us what He did instead: "they went to another village." Not revenge. Not a scene. Just a redirected mission, carried by the same mercy that was headed to a cross all along. Somebody said no to you today. You don't need fire. You need to keep walking in the Spirit you actually belong to — and let Him decide what they deserve.
