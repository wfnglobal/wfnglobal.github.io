Possess Your Possessions

Deliverance gets you out. Holiness keeps you out. But possession is where you finally walk into what God already gave you on paper — and the fire that burns Esau's stubble is the same fire He has put in you.

Word and Faith Network · Obadiah 1:17-18
"But upon mount Zion shall be deliverance, and there shall be holiness; and the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions. And the house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau for stubble, and they shall kindle in them, and devour them; and there shall not be any remaining of the house of Esau; for the LORD hath spoken it."
Obadiah 1:17-18, KJV

Obadiah is the shortest book in the Old Testament — one chapter, twenty-one verses — and almost all of it is about one brother standing on a hill watching the other brother's house burn. When Babylon broke into Jerusalem, Edom, the descendants of Esau, didn't just fail to help. They cheered. They looted the gates. They stood at the crossroads and cut off the people trying to escape, and handed the survivors over to the enemy. God's verdict on that is blunt: "As thou hast done, it shall be done unto thee." And then, right in the middle of judgment, Obadiah turns and speaks to the people who were the victims of it — and what he says to them is one of the most explosive promises in the prophets.

Deliverance Comes First

"But upon mount Zion shall be deliverance." Notice the very first word of the promise: but. Everything before it is what the enemy did. Everything after it is what God will do. Your story is not finished at the part where people gloated over your fall. There is always a "but" with God, and it always starts with deliverance — Him getting you out of what you could never have gotten yourself out of.

But read the order carefully, because the order is the sermon. Deliverance. Then holiness. Then possession. God does not skip a step. He delivers first, because you can't possess anything while you're still in captivity. Too many believers are praying for possession while they're still in chains — asking God for the land while still living in Babylon. Let Him get you out first.

Holiness Is the Hinge

"And there shall be holiness." Holiness is the hinge between being delivered and possessing. It is what keeps you from walking straight back into the thing God just pulled you out of. Israel was delivered from Egypt in a single night, but it took forty years to get Egypt out of Israel — and a whole generation died in the wilderness holding a deliverance they never turned into a possession.

Holiness isn't a list of things you're not allowed to enjoy. It's being set apart so that what God gives you can actually stay with you. Deliverance without holiness is a door that keeps swinging back open. Holiness shuts it behind you.

Possess Your Possessions

"And the house of Jacob shall possess their possessions." Read that phrase slowly. Not "receive new things." Possess their possessions — take hold of what already belonged to them. The land had been promised to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob centuries before. It was theirs on paper the whole time. What they lacked wasn't ownership. It was occupation.

"How long are ye slack to go to possess the land, which the LORD God of your fathers hath given you?"Joshua 18:3, KJV

Joshua had to ask seven tribes that exact question. God had already given it. They just hadn't walked in yet. There are things in your life that heaven settled long ago — peace you're allowed to have, healing that was already paid for, a calling with your name on it, doors God already opened — and you're living beside them instead of inside them. It's time to stop admiring the land from the border.

Fire, Flame and Stubble

Then Obadiah lifts the promise into flame: "the house of Jacob shall be a fire, and the house of Joseph a flame, and the house of Esau for stubble." Esau is not just a nation here. He's a picture. Hebrews calls him a "profane person, who for one morsel of meat sold his birthright" — the man who traded what was eternal for what was immediate. Everything in your life that keeps trading your birthright for a bowl of stew is Esau. And God's word over it is simple: it's stubble.

Stubble doesn't fight fire. It doesn't negotiate with fire. It just burns. And don't miss who God names as the flame: Joseph. The one whose own brothers sold him. The one thrown in a pit and forgotten in a prison. The betrayed one becomes the flame. Whatever was done to you does not get the last word — the very place you were sold out of can become the place God sets you on fire.

The Word for Today

Obadiah ends the verse with five words that settle the matter: "for the LORD hath spoken it." Not if you feel it. Not when circumstances agree. He has spoken it. So take the steps in order. Let Him deliver you from whatever is still holding you captive. Choose holiness so the door stays shut behind you. And then get up and walk into what is already yours — your peace, your health, your family, your calling, your inheritance. You are not stubble. You are the house of Jacob, and you are fire. Possess your possessions. In Jesus' name, amen.
