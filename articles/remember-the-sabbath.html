Remember the Sabbath Day, to Keep It Holy

Six days you labor — but the seventh belongs to the Lord.

Word and Faith Network · Exodus 20
"Remember the sabbath day, to keep it holy. Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work: but the seventh day is the sabbath of the LORD thy God."
Exodus 20:8–10 — KJV

Of the Ten Commandments, this is the one we are most tempted to quietly set aside. We do not usually struggle to agree that murder, theft, and idolatry are wrong. But rest? In a culture that measures worth by output, the fourth commandment lands differently — not as a restriction, but as an interruption we are not sure we can afford.

Yet God did not give this command as an afterthought. He wrote it into the same tablets as "Thou shalt have no other gods before me" and "Thou shalt not kill." Rest, to God, is not a luxury for the weak. It is holy — set apart, consecrated, non-negotiable.

A Pattern Older Than the Law

Before the Sabbath was ever commanded at Sinai, it was built into creation itself. "And on the seventh day God ended his work which he had made; and he rested on the seventh day from all his work which he had made" (Genesis 2:2). God did not rest because He was tired — He is never tired. He rested to establish a rhythm, a pattern His image-bearers would be invited into from the very beginning of the world.

"And God blessed the seventh day, and sanctified it: because that in it he had rested from all his work which God created and made."Genesis 2:3, KJV

This means the Sabbath is not merely a rule for ancient Israel — it is woven into the fabric of time itself. Work and rest were never meant to compete. They were meant to alternate, each one making sense of the other.

Six Days of Labor, Honored

Notice what the commandment actually says before it ever mentions rest: "Six days shalt thou labour, and do all thy work" (v.9). This is not a command that despises work — it assumes it, honors it, and expects it. Scripture never romanticizes idleness. "In all labour there is profit" (Proverbs 14:23). The call to rest is not a call away from diligence; it is a boundary placed around diligence, so that labor never becomes the whole of a person's life.

Without that boundary, work has no edges. It expands to fill every hour it is given. The Sabbath is God's way of saying: your worth was never meant to rest on what you produce. One day in seven, you stop — not because the work is finished, but because you are not the one holding the world together.

Holy Unto the Lord

The word "holy" in verse 8 is the key to the whole command. The Sabbath is not simply a day off — it is a day set apart unto the Lord. This distinguishes biblical rest from mere leisure. The world rests to recover so it can produce more. Scripture calls us to rest as an act of worship — a weekly declaration that we trust God enough to stop.

"Come unto me, all ye that labour and are heavy laden, and I will give you rest."Matthew 11:28, KJV

Jesus, who called Himself "Lord also of the sabbath" (Matthew 12:8), did not abolish this rhythm — He fulfilled it. In Him, rest becomes more than a day on the calendar. It becomes a posture of the heart: ceasing from our own striving to find our sufficiency in Him. "There remaineth therefore a rest to the people of God" (Hebrews 4:9).

The Word for Today

If you have been running without rest, hear this command as the gift it was always meant to be. You do not honor God by exhausting yourself in His name. Build the rhythm back in — a day, an hour, a Sabbath practice that says to your soul and to the watching world: I trust the Lord enough to stop. Six days you labor. One day, you remember whose you are.
