The Lawful Captive Shall Go Free

Some captivity looks legal — you signed for it, you walked into it, the enemy has the paperwork. Heaven's answer is still the same: I will contend with him that contendeth with thee, and I will save thy children.

Word and Faith Network · Isaiah 49:24-25
"Shall the prey be taken from the mighty, or the lawful captive delivered? But thus saith the LORD, Even the captives of the mighty shall be taken away, and the prey of the terrible shall be delivered: for I will contend with him that contendeth with thee, and I will save thy children."
Isaiah 49:24-25, KJV

Before God ever answers the question in verse 24, Zion asks a sadder one in verse 14: "The LORD hath forsaken me, and my Lord hath forgotten me." That is what exile sounds like from the inside. Not a theology debate. A tired sigh. And God's reply is one of the tenderest lines in the whole Bible: "Can a woman forget her sucking child? … yea, they may forget, yet will I not forget thee. Behold, I have graven thee upon the palms of my hands." Your name is not on a list somewhere in heaven. It is carved into His hands. So by the time we reach verse 24, God is not answering a stranger. He is answering a child He refuses to forget.

The Question Behind Your Silence

"Shall the prey be taken from the mighty?" That is not unbelief. That is honesty. Israel was looking at Babylon — the superpower of the age, the empire that didn't give anything back. When a lion has its teeth in something, nobody walks up and asks for it politely. And many of us are asking the same question quietly. Can this addiction really let go of me? Can this debt really be broken? Can my son really come back from where he has gone? Can a family pattern that ran through three generations really stop with me?

Notice that God doesn't scold the question. He answers it. You are allowed to bring Him the thing that looks impossible. What you are not allowed to do is let the size of the captor become the size of your God.

Even the Lawful Captive

Then Isaiah goes further: "or the lawful captive delivered?" That's the part that keeps people up at night. A lawful captive is someone the captor has a legal claim on. Taken in a fair fight. Sold by their own signature. Held because of their own choices. Some of your bondage didn't just happen to you — you walked into it. You said yes to the thing. You signed the contract. You opened the door. And the enemy loves that, because now he isn't just holding you. He's holding you with paperwork.

Here is the gospel: the paperwork has been cancelled. Jesus didn't only overpower the captor; He settled the claim. Every legal accusation the enemy has against you was nailed to a cross and stamped "paid in full." The devil may still be shouting the charges, but he is reading from a document that no longer exists.

"Blotting out the handwriting of ordinances that was against us, which was contrary to us, and took it out of the way, nailing it to his cross; and having spoiled principalities and powers, he made a shew of them openly, triumphing over them in it."Colossians 2:14-15, KJV

I Will Contend With Him

"But thus saith the LORD, Even the captives of the mighty shall be taken away, and the prey of the terrible shall be delivered: for I will contend with him that contendeth with thee." Read that last line again. God doesn't say, "Fight harder." He doesn't say, "Contend with him." He says, "I will contend with him." Your fight just changed hands. The battle that was too big for you has been taken over by Someone for whom it is not big at all.

Mighty meets Almighty. Terrible meets the One who is terrible in majesty. Jesus told us exactly how this works: you don't take a strong man's goods by negotiating with him. You bind him first, then you spoil his house. That's what happened at Calvary. The strong man was bound. Which means everything he has been holding — your peace, your health, your joy, your destiny — is now loot waiting to be carried home.

"Or else how can one enter into a strong man's house, and spoil his goods, except he first bind the strong man? and then he will spoil his house."Matthew 12:29, KJV

And I Will Save Thy Children

And then, as if all that weren't enough, God reaches past you and lays His hand on your house: "and I will save thy children." This is a promise for every praying mother whose child is in a far country, every father whose son has stopped answering the phone, every grandparent who has outlived their own strength to pray. The enemy thought your children were his inheritance. God says they are His. You are not praying into an empty sky; you are praying into a covenant.

Isaiah closes the chapter with the purpose of it all: "and all flesh shall know that I the LORD am thy Saviour and thy Redeemer, the mighty One of Jacob." When God brings your captive home, it won't only be your testimony. It will be a sign to everyone who watched you wait.

Hand Him the Fight

So here is my question for you today: what are you still fighting alone that God has already said He would contend with? Name it. The habit. The diagnosis. The debt. The prodigal. The legal claim the enemy keeps waving in your face. Then hand it over. Not because you are weak, but because He is mighty — mightier than the mighty, more terrible than the terrible.

The prey shall be taken from the mighty. The lawful captive shall be delivered. The One who carved your name into His hands is stepping into your fight, and He has never lost one. Shout it over your house today: my captivity is over, my contender has met his match, and my children are coming home — for thus saith the LORD.
