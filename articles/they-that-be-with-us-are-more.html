They That Be With Us Are More

The servant opened the door at dawn and saw an army around the city. Elisha saw the same army and never flinched — because he saw something else too. One short prayer, LORD, open his eyes, and the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire.

Word and Faith Network · 2 Kings 6:15-17
"And when the servant of the man of God was risen early, and gone forth, behold, an host compassed the city both with horses and chariots. And his servant said unto him, Alas, my master! how shall we do? And he answered, Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them. And Elisha prayed, and said, LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see. And the LORD opened the eyes of the young man; and he saw: and, behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha."
2 Kings 6:15-17, KJV

Some of you woke up this morning surrounded. Not by soldiers — by numbers. The bills outnumber the paycheck. The diagnosis outnumbers the good reports. The people against you outnumber the people for you. You opened your eyes, checked your phone, and the first thing you saw was the size of the problem. If that's you today, I want you to meet a young man who opened a door at dawn and saw an army — and a prophet who saw the same army and went back to his breakfast.

Alas, My Master

The king of Syria had a problem with a preacher. Every time he planned an ambush, Elisha told Israel's king exactly where it would be. So the king of Syria did what fear always does with a man of God: he sent an army to arrest one prophet. "Therefore sent he thither horses, and chariots, and a great host: and they came by night, and compassed the city about." A whole army. For one man and his servant in a small town called Dothan.

And the servant got up early. Good habit. Wrong view. He stepped outside, and "behold, an host compassed the city both with horses and chariots." Every road blocked. Every hill lined with spears. And he ran back inside with the oldest prayer in the world: "Alas, my master! how shall we do?" Hear me — that is not a faithless question. It's an honest one. It's the question you asked in the car park, in the bathroom, at the kitchen table at 2am. How shall we do?

Same Hill, Two Reports

Here's what fascinates me. Elisha and the servant were standing on the same hill, looking at the same army, on the same morning. One said, "Alas." The other said, "Fear not." Same facts. Different sight. The servant wasn't wrong about what he saw. He was wrong about what he couldn't see.

That is the problem with most of our panic. We are not lying about the army. The army is real. The bill is real. The report is real. But we are counting with half the numbers. Fear is just arithmetic with missing information. And Elisha didn't argue with the servant's facts — he finished the sum: "Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

"What shall we then say to these things? If God be for us, who can be against us?"Romans 8:31, KJV

Count Again

Do the math the servant did. Two men. One city. A great host of horses and chariots. Anyone with eyes would say, "We're finished." Now do the math Elisha did. Two men, plus the LORD of hosts, plus every angel He commands. Elisha wasn't being optimistic. He was being accurate. Faith is not pretending the enemy is small. Faith is knowing exactly how big your God is.

And notice: Elisha didn't say, "They that are with us are stronger." He said "more." Heaven didn't just outmuscle Syria. Heaven outnumbered it. You are never the minority in a room where God stands. Whatever is lined up against you this morning, it is the smaller army. It just has better visibility.

LORD, Open His Eyes

Now watch what Elisha prays — because it's not what you'd expect. He doesn't pray, "LORD, send them away." He doesn't pray, "LORD, send help." Help was already there. He prays, "LORD, I pray thee, open his eyes, that he may see." Elisha didn't need God to change the situation. He needed God to change his servant's sight.

"And the LORD opened the eyes of the young man; and he saw: and, behold, the mountain was full of horses and chariots of fire round about Elisha." Full. Not a few angels on the edge. The whole mountain. And here's the part that will preach to you all day: those chariots didn't arrive when he opened his eyes. They were there all night. They were there when he went to bed worried. They were there when he panicked at the door. The protection came before the prayer. The prayer only let him see it.

"The angel of the LORD encampeth round about them that fear him, and delivereth them."Psalm 34:7, KJV

The Army Went Home Blind

And the story doesn't end on the mountain. When the Syrians came down, Elisha prayed again — "Smite this people, I pray thee, with blindness." The ones who came to capture the prophet got led by the prophet, straight into Samaria. And instead of killing them, Elisha told the king to set bread and water before them and send them home. "So the bands of Syria came no more into the land of Israel." The army that came to take him went home fed, humbled, and finished with him. That's what God does with the thing that surrounded you. He doesn't just get you out. He ends the raid.

So here's my question for you today: what are you looking at that God has already surrounded? Name it. The debt. The court date. The child who won't call. The enemy at work who keeps setting traps. You've been staring at their horses all night. It's time to see yours. Greater is He that is in you than he that is in the world. Your problem has chariots — but yours are on fire.

So pray Elisha's prayer over yourself before you walk out that door: "LORD, open my eyes, that I may see." Then say it out loud, the way he said it: "They that be with us are more than they that be with them." Stop counting their horses. Lift your eyes to the mountain. You are not outnumbered. You never were. Walk out surrounded — by fire. In Jesus' name.
