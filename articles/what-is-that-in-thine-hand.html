What Is That in Thine Hand?

Moses had a list of reasons he couldn't go. God didn't argue with one of them. He asked a question — what is that in thine hand? — and turned an old shepherd's stick into the rod of God.

Word and Faith Network · Exodus 4:2-4
"And the LORD said unto him, What is that in thine hand? And he said, A rod. And he said, Cast it on the ground. And he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses fled from before it. And the LORD said unto Moses, Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail. And he put forth his hand, and caught it, and it became a rod in his hand:"
Exodus 4:2-4, KJV

You have a list. Everybody does. The list of reasons you can't. Not educated enough. Not connected enough. Too old, too young, too late, too broke, too ordinary. Moses had a list too — and it was a good one. He was eighty years old. He was a fugitive with a dead Egyptian in his past. He was slow of speech and slow of tongue. He had spent forty years on the backside of the desert keeping sheep that weren't even his. So when God said, "Go to Pharaoh," Moses pulled out his list and started reading. God didn't argue with one item on it. He asked a question instead: "What is that in thine hand?"

God Starts With What You Have

Notice what God doesn't ask. He doesn't ask, "What do you wish you had?" He doesn't ask, "What does your brother Aaron have?" He doesn't ask for a résumé, an army, or a plan. He points at the most ordinary thing in Moses' life — a stick. A shepherd's rod. Worn smooth by forty years of his own grip. Nothing special. Nothing anybody would steal. And heaven says: that. Start with that.

This is the pattern all through Scripture. A widow with a pot of oil. A boy with five loaves and two small fishes. David with a sling and five smooth stones. Shamgar with an ox goad. Every time, God walks right past what people don't have and puts His finger on what they do. You keep praying, "Lord, give me more." He keeps answering, "What is that in thine hand?" The breakthrough you're waiting for may already be sitting in your grip — and you've been treating it like a stick.

"But God hath chosen the foolish things of the world to confound the wise; and God hath chosen the weak things of the world to confound the things which are mighty."1 Corinthians 1:27, KJV

Cast It Down

"Cast it on the ground." Before God could use the rod, Moses had to let go of it. Think about what that rod was. His security. His livelihood. His walking stick, his weapon against wolves, the one thing in the wilderness he could lean on. It was the symbol of the small, safe life he had settled into after his big dreams in Egypt fell apart. And God says: drop it. Put it down in front of Me.

Some of you are gripping what you have so tightly that God can't do anything with it. Your gift. Your money. Your plans. Your past. You're holding it the way you've always held it — for you, for survival, for safety. So here's my question for you today: what are you holding that you have never actually laid down before God? Not "used for God." Laid down. Because nothing becomes holy in your hand until it has first been surrendered out of it.

The Serpent on the Ground

"And he cast it on the ground, and it became a serpent; and Moses fled from before it." I love the honesty of the Bible. The man God is about to send against the most powerful king on earth runs from his own stick. Don't skip that. When you lay something down before God, the first thing you see is not always the miracle. Sometimes it's the thing you were afraid of. The serpent — the emblem coiled on Pharaoh's own crown, the old enemy from the garden. And Moses runs from it, the same way he ran from Egypt forty years before.

Some of you have been running from the same serpent for decades. The fear. The failure. The voice that says you will never be enough. And God, in His kindness, puts it on the ground right in front of you — not to torment you, but to show you something: it has no authority over a life God has called.

Take It by the Tail

"Put forth thine hand, and take it by the tail." Every shepherd knows you never grab a snake by the tail. You grab it behind the head, or it turns and bites you. God tells Moses to do the one thing that makes no sense. Reach for the dangerous end. Pick up what you ran from. And the moment Moses obeys, the serpent goes stiff — "it became a rod in his hand."

That is faith. Not the absence of fear — obedience while the fear is still on the ground hissing at you. You don't conquer what you keep running from. You conquer it by reaching for it at God's word. The serpent didn't turn back because Moses was brave. It turned back because Moses obeyed. Your boldness is not the miracle. His word is.

"And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness."2 Corinthians 12:9, KJV

The Rod of God

Now watch what happens to that stick. Sixteen verses later, the Bible calls it something new: "And Moses took the rod of God in his hand." Same wood. Same length. Same scratches from forty years in the desert. But it is not Moses' rod anymore. It is the rod of God. That rod will stand before Pharaoh. That rod will stretch over the Red Sea until the waters stand up like a wall. That rod will strike a rock and water will pour out for a nation in the wilderness. That rod will be lifted on a hilltop while Israel prevails over Amalek.

Not because it was a great stick. Because it was a surrendered one. What you lay down before God never comes back the same. Your ordinary becomes His instrument. Your small becomes His sign. Your "it's only this" becomes the thing that parts the sea.

So stop despising what's in your hand. Stop waiting on what you don't have. Look down. Name it — the skill, the voice, the kitchen, the phone, the twenty dollars, the testimony nobody has heard yet. Cast it down before Him today. Reach for the thing you've been running from. And pick up the rod of God. Pharaoh is not waiting on your qualifications. Heaven is waiting on your surrender. Go — and take the rod with you. In Jesus' name.
