When I Fall, I Shall Arise

The enemy laughed when you went down, and some of the fall was your own fault. Micah doesn't deny either one — he just refuses to stay on the floor: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the LORD shall be a light unto me.

Word and Faith Network · Micah 7:8-9
"Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy: when I fall, I shall arise; when I sit in darkness, the LORD shall be a light unto me. I will bear the indignation of the LORD, because I have sinned against him, until he plead my cause, and execute judgment for me: he will bring me forth to the light, and I shall behold his righteousness."
Micah 7:8-9, KJV

Some of you didn't just fall this year. You fell in public. The marriage ended and everybody heard. The business closed and the whole street knew. The habit you said was dead came back, and somebody saw. And as painful as the fall was, it wasn't the worst part. The worst part was the smile on certain faces. The whispers. The ones who almost seemed glad. If you are reading this from the floor this morning, I have a word from a prophet who wrote from the floor too — and he refused to stay there.

Rejoice Not Against Me

Micah lived in a collapsing nation. The leaders were corrupt. The courts were bought. Families were turning on one another — "a man's enemies are the men of his own house." It was dark, and it was getting darker. And right in the middle of that ruin, Micah turns around and speaks to the enemy who is enjoying the show: "Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy."

Hear that. He doesn't whisper it. He doesn't apologize for still being alive. He looks the gloating straight in the face and says: don't celebrate yet. Your party is premature. You saw me go down. You have not seen how this ends. Somebody needs to say that today to the voice in your head, to the devil, to the report, to the people who wrote you off: rejoice not against me. You are counting me out too early.

When I Fall, I Shall Arise

Notice the word Micah uses. Not if I fall. When I fall. The man of God isn't pretending he'll never hit the ground. He's telling you what happens after he does. Falling is real. Falling is human. But falling is not finishing. The fall is an event. It is not your identity. It is not your address. It is not your ending.

The difference between the righteous and the ruined is not that one falls and the other doesn't. They both fall. The difference is that one gets up. And he gets up not because he is strong, but because there is a God who refuses to let His people stay on the ground. You were not built to live down there. Count your falls if you want to. Heaven is counting your risings.

"For a just man falleth seven times, and riseth up again: but the wicked shall fall into mischief."Proverbs 24:16, KJV

When I Sit in Darkness

Then Micah goes further: "when I sit in darkness, the LORD shall be a light unto me." Sit. Not pass through. Sit. Some darkness you walk through quickly. Other darkness you sit in — for months. The diagnosis that stays. The silence that drags on. The depression that settles over the house like weather. Micah is honest: sometimes you are sitting there a while.

But look at what he does not say. He does not say, "when the darkness leaves, then the LORD will be my light." He says while I am sitting in it — right there, before anything changes — the LORD shall be a light unto me. God doesn't wait for your situation to brighten before He shows up. He becomes the light in the dark room. You may not see the way out yet. But you are not sitting there alone.

Because I Have Sinned

Now here is the line most people skip, and it's the one that sets you free: "I will bear the indignation of the LORD, because I have sinned against him." Micah doesn't blame the enemy for everything. He owns his part. Some of what happened to you, you didn't deserve. But some of it — if we're honest — came through doors we opened ourselves. And the enemy loves that part, because he uses your guilt to keep you on the floor. "You did this. You don't get to get up."

But listen to what comes next: "until he plead my cause, and execute judgment for me." The very God I sinned against becomes the One who takes my case. That is the gospel in the Old Testament. "If any man sin, we have an advocate with the Father, Jesus Christ the righteous." Your accuser has a case. But your Advocate has a cross. You are allowed to own your mistake and still rise — because the One who pleads your cause has already paid for it.

"Though he fall, he shall not be utterly cast down: for the LORD upholdeth him with his hand."Psalm 37:24, KJV

He Will Bring Me Forth to the Light

And then the promise: "he will bring me forth to the light, and I shall behold his righteousness." Not "I will claw my way out." He will bring me forth. The same God who let you feel the floor is the God who will lift you off it. And the next verse finishes the story: the enemy who said, "Where is the LORD thy God?" will see it — and shame will cover the mouth that mocked you. The ones who watched you fall will watch you rise.

So here's my question for you today: how long have you been sitting where you fell? A month? A year? Ten years? Some of you have decorated the floor. You've made peace with the dark. You've accepted the enemy's commentary as the final word. Not today. Today you look at every voice that rejoiced over your fall, and you answer the way Micah answered.

Say it out loud: "Rejoice not against me, O mine enemy. When I fall, I shall arise. When I sit in darkness, the LORD shall be a light unto me." Then get up. Not because you feel strong. Because He is pleading your cause, and He is bringing you forth to the light. Knocked down is not knocked out. You fell. Now rise. In Jesus' name.
