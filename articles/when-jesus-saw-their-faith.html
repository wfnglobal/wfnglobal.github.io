When Jesus Saw Their Faith

The house was packed and the door was jammed, so four friends climbed onto the roof, broke it open, and let a paralyzed man down at Jesus' feet. Mark records one line that should change how you pray for the people you love: when Jesus saw their faith.

Word and Faith Network · Mark 2:3-5
"And they come unto him, bringing one sick of the palsy, which was borne of four. And when they could not come nigh unto him for the press, they uncovered the roof where he was: and when they had broken it up, they let down the bed wherein the sick of the palsy lay. When Jesus saw their faith, he said unto the sick of the palsy, Son, thy sins be forgiven thee."
Mark 2:3-5, KJV

There is a man in Capernaum who cannot get to Jesus on his own. He can't walk. He can't push through. He may not even have the strength to ask. Everything he receives today depends on four people who decided his problem was their problem. Maybe that's you this Saturday — flat on the mat, too tired to pray, too far gone to fight the crowd. Or maybe you're one of the four, and your arms are aching from carrying somebody you love toward a Jesus they can't reach yet. Either way, I want to take you up on a roof. Because this is the day God shows us what happens when friends stop standing at the door and start digging through the ceiling.

Borne of Four

"And they come unto him, bringing one sick of the palsy, which was borne of four." Mark doesn't give us a single name. Not the man on the mat. Not the four men holding the corners. Heaven recorded the faith and left the names off. Palsy means paralysis — a body that will not answer. He couldn't come to Jesus, so Jesus' people came to him.

Hear me. Some people will never make it to Jesus on their own legs. The addiction has them flat. The depression has them pinned. The grief has turned every limb to stone. They are not going to stroll into church next Sunday. Somebody is going to have to carry them. And the question this text asks is not, "Do they deserve it?" The question is, "Who's got a corner?"

"Two are better than one; because they have a good reward for their labour. For if they fall, the one will lift up his fellow: but woe to him that is alone when he falleth; for he hath not another to help him up."Ecclesiastes 4:9-10, KJV

No Room, Not So Much as About the Door

Word had spread that Jesus was in the house, and "there was no room to receive them, no, not so much as about the door." The four friends get there sweating, the mat sagging between them — and the doorway is jammed solid. Shoulders. Elbows. Backs turned. The crowd that came for Jesus was the very thing standing between a broken man and Jesus.

That's a hard word, but it's true: sometimes the biggest obstacle between a hurting person and the Savior is a room full of people who already have a seat. And the four had every excuse in the book. "We tried." "It's too crowded." "We'll come back next week." Most of us would have set the mat down by the door, said a quick prayer over it, and called that ministry.

Faith Climbs

But faith doesn't stop at the door. Faith climbs. Up the outside stairs. Onto the flat roof. "They uncovered the roof where he was: and when they had broken it up, they let down the bed wherein the sick of the palsy lay." Picture it. Plaster falling on the scribes. Dust in the preacher's hair. A crack of daylight, then a hole, then a mat coming down on four ropes into the middle of the sermon.

They didn't wait for an invitation. They didn't ask permission. They didn't care how it looked. Four men tore through a roof with their bare hands because they were sure of one thing: if we can just get him to Jesus, Jesus will do the rest. That isn't recklessness. That's faith with dirt under its fingernails.

So here's my question for you today: who are you carrying — and where did you set them down? The prodigal you quietly stopped praying for. The friend you gave up on after the third relapse. The marriage you already buried in your heart. You got them as far as the door, the crowd was thick, and you went home. Go back. Pick up your corner. There's a roof you haven't tried yet.

He Saw Their Faith

"When Jesus saw their faith." Stop right there. Jesus saw it. Faith you can see looks like rope burns and broken tiles. It looks like a hole in somebody's ceiling. And notice whose faith He saw — their faith. The text never tells us what the man on the mat believed. Jesus looked up at four dusty faces framed in a hole in the roof, and He moved on what He saw in them.

Somebody needs this today: your faith can reach where your loved one's legs can't. Your prayer can carry a child who isn't praying. Your fasting can lower a husband who won't come near a church. No one is saved on someone else's believing — that man still had to rise when Jesus spoke. But this is the oldest truth of intercession: Jesus responds to people who are carried by people who refuse to stop believing.

Son, Thy Sins Be Forgiven Thee

And then Jesus says the last thing anybody expected. Not "be healed." Not "rise." "Son, thy sins be forgiven thee." The friends brought him for his legs; Jesus started with his heart. He went to the root before He touched the symptom. Before He fixed what everybody could see, He fixed what nobody could. And He called him "Son" — a man who couldn't move got adopted before he got up.

"Blessed is he whose transgression is forgiven, whose sin is covered."Psalm 32:1, KJV

Then, so the scribes would know exactly who they were dealing with, Jesus said, "Arise, and take up thy bed" — and the man "went forth before them all." The same crowd that blocked the door had to make room for him on the way out. The mat that carried him, he now carried. And the whole house said, "We never saw it on this fashion."

So today, pick up a corner. Name one person you are carrying to Jesus, and refuse to leave them at the door. Bear ye one another's burdens. If the door is jammed, go up. If the roof is in the way, break it open. And if you're the one on the mat this morning — too weak to pray, too tired to push — let somebody carry you. Say it out loud: "I will not leave them at the door. I'm taking my corner. I'm going up on the roof. And when Jesus sees our faith, He will speak forgiveness — and they will rise." Then go find your rope, child of God. No roof is stronger than faith that's willing to climb. In Jesus' name.
