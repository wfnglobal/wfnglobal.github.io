When the Thorn Stays

Paul prayed three times for the thorn to leave, and it stayed. But heaven's answer wasn't silence — it was grace enough to carry it, and the power of Christ resting on the very place he felt weakest.

Word and Faith Network · 2 Corinthians 12:8-10
"For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me. And he said unto me, My grace is sufficient for thee: for my strength is made perfect in weakness. Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me. Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong."
2 Corinthians 12:8-10, KJV

There is a prayer you have prayed so many times you could pray it in your sleep. Take it away, Lord. Heal it. Fix it. Lift it. You fasted over it. You stood in prayer lines about it. You wrote it on the back of an envelope and pushed it into a church offering bag. And this morning it is still there. The pain is still there. The weakness is still there. The thing you begged God to remove is sitting at the breakfast table with you again. If that's you, I want you to know something before we go one line further: you are in very good company. The apostle Paul prayed that prayer too. And what God told him might be the most important word you hear this year.

Thrice

"For this thing I besought the Lord thrice, that it might depart from me." Don't rush past who is talking. This is Paul. The man who was caught up into the third heaven. The man whose handkerchiefs carried healing to the sick. The man who raised Eutychus from the dead and shook a viper off his hand into the fire. If anybody had the faith to pray something off, it was Paul.

And he prayed. Once. Twice. Three times. Not casually — "besought" is the language of a man on his knees, pleading. And the thorn stayed. Hear me: a thorn that stays is not proof that your faith is small. It is not proof that God is angry. It is not proof that you are not called. Paul had the greatest revelation of grace in the New Testament, and he still had a thorn. Some of the most anointed people you will ever meet are walking around with something God didn't take away. Stop letting the thorn preach a sermon about you that God never preached.

No Is Not Silence

Here is what most people miss. Heaven did not ignore Paul. "And he said unto me." God answered. It just wasn't the answer Paul asked for. Paul asked for removal. God gave him a revelation: "My grace is sufficient for thee."

So here's my question for you today: have you mistaken a different answer for no answer? You've been waiting for the thorn to leave, and while you were waiting, grace has been carrying you. You got up. You went to work. You raised those children. You kept your mind. You are still standing. That is not luck. That is not willpower. That is sufficient grace, showing up every single morning, doing exactly what God said it would do. Not grace that is barely enough. Grace that is enough. Not grace for some of it. Grace for thee — personally, specifically, for this thorn, on this day.

"But we have this treasure in earthen vessels, that the excellency of the power may be of God, and not of us."2 Corinthians 4:7, KJV

Made Perfect in Weakness

Then God says something that turns everything upside down. "My strength is made perfect in weakness." Not made perfect in your talent. Not made perfect in your good days. Made perfect — brought to its full, finished display — in weakness. God is not working in spite of the weak place in your life. He is working right through it.

Jacob wrestled with God all night and walked away with a blessing and a limp. He halted upon his thigh for the rest of his life. And every time Israel saw that old man limping, they remembered: that's the one who met God face to face and lived. The limp became the testimony. Your weakness is not the end of your story. It's the window people will look through to see that your strength was never yours in the first place. Earthen vessels crack. That's how the light gets out.

Let the Power Rest

And look at how Paul responds. He stops begging and starts boasting. "Most gladly therefore will I rather glory in my infirmities, that the power of Christ may rest upon me." That word "rest" carries the picture of a tent being pitched over someone. Paul is saying: the power of Christ comes and camps over the very place I feel weakest. Where the thorn is, there the tent is. Where the ache is, there the glory settles.

That means you can stop hiding it. Stop pretending you are fine. Stop measuring your walk with God by whether the thorn is gone. Keep praying — Paul did, and God still heals, still delivers, still removes. But do not hold your peace hostage to the answer you want. Some battles God ends. Some battles God carries you through. Either way, the power of Christ is resting on you, and nothing the enemy sends can buffet you out from under that tent.

"I can do all things through Christ which strengtheneth me."Philippians 4:13, KJV

When I Am Weak, Then Am I Strong

Now listen to how Paul ends it. "Therefore I take pleasure in infirmities, in reproaches, in necessities, in persecutions, in distresses for Christ's sake: for when I am weak, then am I strong." That isn't a man who gave up. That's a man who found out where the real power was. The reproach couldn't stop him. The lack couldn't stop him. The pressure couldn't stop him. Because every time he came to the end of himself, he ran straight into the beginning of Christ.

So today, I'm not asking you to pretend the thorn doesn't hurt. I'm asking you to stop letting it have the last word. Take the weak place — the diagnosis, the debt, the grief, the habit you're still fighting, the thing that keeps you up at night — and lift it up in front of God. Say it out loud: "Lord, Your grace is sufficient for me. Your strength is made perfect right here. Let the power of Christ rest upon me." Then get up and walk in it, limp and all. The thorn may still be in your side. But the tent of His power is over your head. And when you are weak — hear me — then are you strong. In Jesus' name.
