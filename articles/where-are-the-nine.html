Where Are the Nine?

Ten lepers cried for mercy, and all ten were cleansed on the road. Only one turned back to fall at Jesus' feet — and Jesus asked a question that still stings: were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?

Word and Faith Network · Luke 17:15-17
"And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan. And Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?"
Luke 17:15-17, KJV

Some of the most dangerous people in church aren't backslidden. They're blessed. They prayed, God answered, and they moved on so fast they never came back to say so. If you've been praying, waiting, pressing — and especially if God has already done something for you this year that you've stopped mentioning — I want to take you to a village road on the border of Samaria and Galilee, where ten men got the same miracle, nine got exactly what they asked for, and only one got what Jesus actually came to give. Jesus noticed the difference. And on this Thursday He's asking the same question He asked that day: where are the nine?

Ten Voices, One Distance

As Jesus entered a certain village, "there met him ten men that were lepers, which stood afar off." Afar off — because the law told them to. A leper tore his clothes, covered his lip, cried "Unclean, unclean," and lived "without the camp," alone. No handshake. No hug. No seat at the family table. Leprosy didn't just eat your skin. It ate your name, your house, your place in the room.

And notice: there was a Samaritan in that group. Jews and Samaritans didn't eat together, didn't worship together, didn't share a bucket at a well. But suffering is a great leveller. When everybody's outside the camp, nobody's checking passports. Misery had made them one congregation — and with one voice, from a distance, they lifted it up: "Jesus, Master, have mercy on us."

As They Went

Jesus didn't touch them. Didn't spit, didn't pray, didn't make mud. He gave one instruction: "Go shew yourselves unto the priests." That was the procedure for a leper who was already clean — and they weren't. Not yet. Their skin still said leper. Their mirror still said leper. And they turned around and walked anyway. "And it came to pass, that, as they went, they were cleansed."

Hear me. Some of you are waiting to feel healed before you'll move. But heaven loves to meet you on the road, not at the starting line. The miracle was in the walking. Somewhere between "go" and "the priest's house," ten men looked down and saw new skin.

One Turned Back

Then the story splits. "And one of them, when he saw that he was healed, turned back, and with a loud voice glorified God, and fell down on his face at his feet, giving him thanks: and he was a Samaritan." The same voice that cried "have mercy" from a distance now shouts glory up close. The man who had to stand afar off now lies at Jesus' feet. Healing gave him his skin back. Gratitude gave him proximity.

And look who it was. The outsider. The one with the wrong accent and the wrong temple. The one nobody expected to have manners. Luke drops it at the end of the sentence like a gavel — "and he was a Samaritan." Sometimes the person with the least religious background has the most honest response. He didn't know the proper channels. He just knew who had healed him.

"Bless the LORD, O my soul, and forget not all his benefits: Who forgiveth all thine iniquities; who healeth all thy diseases."Psalm 103:2-3, KJV

Where Are the Nine?

"And Jesus answering said, Were there not ten cleansed? but where are the nine?" Jesus can count. He knew exactly how many He healed, and He knew exactly how many came back. Heaven keeps the receipts. Not to shame you — but because God is not a vending machine. He's a Person. And persons notice when nobody says thank you.

Now be careful, because the nine weren't wicked. They were obedient. They went exactly where Jesus told them to go. They were on their way to church! That's what makes this so sobering — ingratitude can look perfectly religious. You can follow every instruction and still forget the Instructor. The nine were busy with the procedure. The one was overwhelmed by the Person. The nine went to get certified. The one went to get close.

So here's my question for you today: which group are you walking with? When did you last go back? Not to ask — just to thank. The job you prayed for that's now just a job. The child you begged God for who's now just "the kids." The breath in your lungs, the roof over you, the sin He wiped and never brought up again. We are loud when we need Him and quiet when we've got it. We pray in a crisis and coast in a blessing. Blessing that isn't acknowledged quietly turns into entitlement — and entitlement is just ingratitude that's had time to settle in.

Cleansed or Made Whole

Here's the part most people miss. All ten were cleansed. But to the one who came back, Jesus said something He never said to the nine: "Arise, go thy way: thy faith hath made thee whole." Cleansed is skin. Whole is everything. The nine got their bodies back. The one got his body back and his soul restored, his shame lifted, his relationship with God begun. Nine got a miracle. One got the Master.

Thanksgiving is not a closing ceremony for a miracle. It's the door to the next one. Gratitude doesn't just say thank you for what God did — it brings you back into the presence of who God is. And that's where wholeness lives.

"In every thing give thanks: for this is the will of God in Christ Jesus concerning you."1 Thessalonians 5:18, KJV

So don't just be cleansed today. Be whole. Turn around. Go back to the feet of Jesus before you go anywhere else. Name what He's done — out loud, specifically, with a loud voice if you have to. Thank Him for the healing, and then thank Him for the Healer. Say it: "Lord, I am not one of the nine. I came back. I see what You did, and I give You glory." And then watch Him lift you off your face and say the words the nine never heard: arise — thy faith hath made thee whole. In Jesus' name.
