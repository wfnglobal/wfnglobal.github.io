The breakthrough that only shows up on try number seven ☁️🔥

Today's sermon: "Go Again Seven Times" (1 Kings 18:43-44)

Six times the servant climbed, looked at a flat, empty sea, and came back down to say the same true thing: there is nothing. Elijah never argued with the report. He just said go again, like the first six nothings were simply the toll for reaching the seventh look.

That's the part of breakthrough nobody frames and hangs on the wall — the climbing that produces nothing you can point to, five and six times in a row, while the sky stays exactly the way it's always been. But the cloud that finally rises is only a hand's width wide, small enough to miss if you quit on climb number six.

"For the vision is yet for an appointed time, but at the end it shall speak, and not lie: though it tarry, wait for it; because it will surely come, it will not tarry." (Habakkuk 2:3, KJV)

✨ Inside the full teaching:
• Why Elijah never rebuked the servant for bringing bad news six times
• What the "cloud like a man's hand" teaches about small, easy-to-miss answers
• How "go again" becomes "get thy chariot ready" without a single sign in between

What's your sixth nothing right now? Type "I go again" and let's believe for the seventh. 👇
