=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; around midday) ===
They That Be With Us Are More

A king sent an army to arrest one preacher.

Horses. Chariots. A great host.
They came by night and surrounded the town of Dothan.

Next morning, Elisha's servant got up early.
Good habit.
He opened the door.

Every road blocked. Every hill lined with spears.

He ran back inside: "Alas, my master! how shall we do?"

Same question you asked at 2am last week.

Here's what gets me.
Elisha was standing on the same hill.
Looking at the same army.
And he wasn't scared.

He didn't deny the army. He finished the math.

Then he prayed one short sentence.
It wasn't "Lord, send them away."
It wasn't "Lord, send help."

It was stranger than both — and it's the prayer you need before you open your own door tomorrow…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; stands alone — early afternoon or evening) ===
Some of you have been doing math all week.

Rent minus paycheck.
Days until the results come back.
How many people are on your side at work. (You counted. It was a short list.)
How many times you've said "I'm fine" this week. (You stopped counting.)

Every time you run the numbers, you lose.

And somewhere around Thursday a quiet voice started saying,
"Face it. It's you against everybody. And everybody's winning."

That's a lie. But it's a convincing one, because the numbers look real.

Let me take you to a hill called Dothan.

A king wanted one prophet arrested. So he sent an entire army.
Horses. Chariots. Overnight.
They surrounded the whole town.

Elisha's servant gets up early, opens the door, coffee in hand (okay, probably not coffee) —
and there's an army staring back at him.

He runs inside. "Alas, my master! how shall we do?"

Just like you.
You opened your banking app. Your inbox. Your group chat.
And there it was. The army.

Elisha looks at the same army and says,
"Fear not: for they that be with us are more than they that be with them."

The servant must've looked around the room like,
"...Sir, it's two of us."

So Elisha prays. Not "Lord, make them leave."
"LORD, open his eyes, that he may see."

And the servant looks again —
the mountain is full of horses and chariots of fire. All around them.

Here's what changed everything:

Those chariots didn't arrive when he prayed. They were there all night. He just couldn't see them.

Fear isn't lying about the army. It's lying about the headcount.

You're not outnumbered. You're under-informed.

So today:
Stop rereading the bad report.
Stop counting their horses.
Close the calculator in your head for five minutes.
Lift your eyes and ask God to show you what's standing on your side.

Then say it out loud:
"LORD, open my eyes. They that be with me are more than they that be with them."

Walk out that door.
Their army has horses. Yours is on fire.

In Jesus' name.
