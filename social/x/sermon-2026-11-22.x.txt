=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; around midday) ===
He Shall Doubtless Come Again

Picture a farmer in a bad year.

The barn is nearly empty.
There's one bag of grain left.

That bag is supper.
For his wife. For his kids. For tonight.

It's also the only seed he has for next year.

So he does the hardest thing a hungry man can do.
He walks past the kitchen and out into the field.
And he throws supper into the dirt.

Handful after handful.
Crying the whole way down the row.

Psalm 126 doesn't edit his tears out of the picture.
It writes them into the promise.

And then it adds one word.
A word heaven doesn't spend lightly.
A word that changes what those tears mean — and what yours mean, if you've been obeying God with wet eyes lately…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; stands alone — early afternoon or evening) ===
It's Sunday.

Somewhere this morning, someone stood in church, sang "Great Is Thy Faithfulness," and hoped nobody noticed their eyes.

Maybe that was you.

You kept showing up to the marriage.
You kept giving when the account said don't.
You kept texting the son who leaves you on read.
You kept doing the job right for a boss who still can't pronounce your name.

You've been doing the right thing for a long time.
And crying through most of it.

Lately a voice has started asking,
"What's the point? You plant and plant, and nothing comes up."

That voice sounds wise. It isn't. It's just tired.

Let me take you to a field in Psalm 126.

A bad year. One bag of grain left.
That bag is tonight's supper — and next year's seed.

The farmer walks past the kitchen and into the field.
And he throws supper into the dirt.
Crying the whole row.

His neighbour leans on the fence like,
"...You know that was dinner, right?"

Just like you.
Everybody can see what your obedience is costing you.
Nobody can see what it's growing.

But God wrote this over that farmer:
"He that goeth forth and weepeth, bearing precious seed, shall doubtless come again with rejoicing, bringing his sheaves with him."

Doubtless. Not maybe. Not "if the economy behaves."

Here's what I need you to keep:

Tears and seed can leave the house together. God never said stop crying before you sow.

Buried is not the same as gone. Your seed isn't missing — it's underground, working a shift you can't see.

You walked out with a handful. You're coming back with an armful.

So today:
Don't eat your seed because you're hungry.
Don't quit the row because you're sad.
Cry if you need to. Then reach back into the bag.

And say it out loud:
"I sow in tears. I shall reap in joy. I shall doubtless come again."

Wipe your face. Pick up the bag.
The harvest already has your name on it.

In Jesus' name.
