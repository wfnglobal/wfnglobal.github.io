=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; around midday) ===
Master, Carest Thou Not?

Twelve men in a fishing boat.
At least four of them fishermen by trade.

They knew that lake.
They knew what a bad night looked like.

This was worse.

The waves weren't splashing in anymore.
They were filling the boat.

And the professionals were panicking.

Meanwhile, in the back of the boat —
Jesus. Asleep. On a pillow.

So they shook Him awake and said the thing every frightened heart has thought at 3 a.m.:
"Master, don't You care that we're going under?"

Here's what most people miss.
They weren't in that storm because they disobeyed Him.
They were in it because they obeyed Him.

And before they ever untied the boat, Jesus had said one sentence that should have changed the whole night…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; stands alone — early afternoon or evening) ===
Last night, around 3 a.m., you were awake again.

Not because of noise.
Because of thoughts.

The payment that's due Friday.
The scan they want to "take another look at."
The kid who said "I'm fine" in that voice.
The job that might not be there in January.

You prayed about it. Twice.
You did everything God told you to do.

And the water is still coming over the side.

The worst part isn't the storm.
It's the silence.
Heaven feels like it put you on hold.

So a quiet question sneaks in:
"Does God even care about this?"

Let me take you to a boat on the Sea of Galilee.

Jesus told His disciples, "Let us pass over unto the other side." His idea. His boat trip.

Halfway across, a storm hits so hard the boat starts filling up.
These are fishermen. They are panicking.

And Jesus?
In the back. Asleep. On a pillow.

Somebody went and got Him a pillow, and nobody went and got Him a bucket.

They shake Him awake:
"Master, carest thou not that we perish?"

Just like you.
You're not asking for a miracle. You're asking if you matter.

Then the Bible says, "He arose."
He looks at the wind and tells it off.
He looks at the sea and says, "Peace, be still."
And the sea goes quiet like a dog that's been told "sit."

Here's what I need you to keep:

He's not asleep because He doesn't care. He's asleep because the storm has to obey Him.

You didn't get in this storm by missing God. Sometimes you get in it by following Him.

Don't measure His love by the noise of the wind. Measure it by the nails.

So tonight:
Go back to what He told you before the storm started.
Stop asking if He cares.
Start telling the storm who's in your boat.

And say it out loud:
"Master, I know You care for me. Peace, be still. I'm going over to the other side."

Lie down. Close your eyes.
If Jesus can sleep in this storm, so can you.

In Jesus' name.
