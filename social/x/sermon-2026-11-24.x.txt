=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; around midday) ===
Wilt Thou Be Made Whole?

There was a pool in Jerusalem where hurting people went to wait.
Blind. Lame. Withered.
Five porches full of them.

And one man had been lying there thirty-eight years.

Not a bad month. Not a hard season.
Thirty-eight years.

He was sick before half the people walking past him were born.
Nobody asked how he was doing anymore.
They just knew where to find him.

Then Jesus walked in.
Past the porches. Past the crowd.
And stopped at his mat.

He didn't say, "Be healed."
He asked a question that sounds almost rude:
"Wilt thou be made whole?"

Of course he wants it. He's lying at a healing pool.
Right?

Not necessarily.
After thirty-eight years, hope gets expensive. Some people quietly stop paying for it.

And the man's answer proves it.
Jesus asked him what he wanted.
He answered with something else entirely…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; stands alone — early afternoon or evening) ===
Some of you have a spot on the couch that's shaped like you now.

Not because you're lazy.
Because you're tired.

The back pain that's been "temporary" since 2019.
The degree you were going to finish "next fall."
The prayer you stopped praying out loud because you got embarrassed it was still the same prayer.
The friends who got married, got promoted, got healed — while you clapped from the same seat.

You're happy for them. You really are.
You just wish, once, it would be your turn.

Somewhere along the way you stopped saying "when."
Now you say, "It is what it is."

That isn't peace.
That's giving up with good manners.

Let me take you to a pool in Jerusalem called Bethesda.

Five porches full of sick people, all waiting for the water to move.
And one man who'd been lying there thirty-eight years.

Thirty-eight. His mat had seniority.

Jesus walks past the whole crowd, stops over him, and asks:
"Wilt thou be made whole?"

And the man says... not yes.
He says, "Sir, I have no man to put me into the pool. Every time I try, somebody else gets there first."

Just like you.
Jesus asked what you want.
You answered with who didn't help you.

Here's the funny part.
He was telling the Man who walks on water that he couldn't reach the water.

Jesus didn't call for a team. Didn't wait for the pool to bubble.
He said, "Rise, take up thy bed, and walk."
And immediately — thirty-eight years, one sentence — the man walked out carrying the thing that used to carry him.

Here's what I need you to keep:

You may have no man. You have the Son of Man.

The length of your wait doesn't set the length of your miracle.

Don't keep the mat as a backup plan. Carry it out as a receipt.

So today:
Stop rehearsing who didn't show up.
Answer the question He actually asked.
Put one foot on the floor before you feel ready.

And say it out loud:
"Yes, Lord. I will be made whole. I rise, I take up my bed, and I walk."

Get up. Fold the mat.
Your spot by the pool is about to be available.

In Jesus' name.
