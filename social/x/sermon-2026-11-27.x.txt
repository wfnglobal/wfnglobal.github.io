=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; around midday) ===
Thou Shalt Eat Bread at My Table

He was five years old when the bad news came.

His nurse grabbed him and ran.
She tripped.
He fell.

And a prince woke up a cripple.
Not for anything he did.
For somebody else's panic.

He grew up in a town called Lo-debar.
It means "no pasture."
Nothing growing. Nobody looking.
The perfect place to disappear.

Because when a new king takes the throne, the old king's grandson doesn't get a pension.
He gets hunted.

So he kept his head down for years.
Flinched every time a horse came up the road.

Then one day, the horses stopped at his door.
The King had sent for him.

They carried him into the palace. He fell on his face and waited for the sword.

And the King opened his mouth and said the last thing he ever expected to hear…

=== C) SHARE POST (reply under the poster post, or post on its own) ===
What are you still calling yourself that God never called you?

Failure. Too far gone. Damaged goods.

Mephibosheth sat in the King's palace and still said, "What is thy servant, that thou shouldest look upon such a dead dog as I am?"

The King's answer? "Thou shalt eat bread at my table continually." — 2 Samuel 9:7

Humility that argues with grace isn't humility. It's unbelief in sackcloth.

Pray: Lord, I stop calling myself what You've never called me. I take my seat.

Send this to someone who's been hiding from God.

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; stands alone — early afternoon or evening) ===
Today half the world is standing in a line for a deal.

Doors open at 6. Limited stock. First come, first served.

And some of you know that feeling a little too well.
Not for TVs.
For love.

You've spent years trying to earn a spot.
Show up early. Work twice as hard. Never be a problem.
Apologize before anyone asks.

You've been hurt by things you didn't even cause.
A parent who left. A house that fell apart. A church that dropped you.
You limped out of it, found a quiet corner, and decided the best you could hope for was to be left alone.

And here's the lie that settles in:
"People like me don't get a seat at the table."

I want to take you to a palace in Jerusalem.

There's a man named Mephibosheth.
When he was five, his nurse ran in a panic, dropped him, and he never walked right again.
Grandson of the old king — which in those days meant you hide, or you die.
So he hid. In a town called Lo-debar. Literally "no pasture."

Just like you, he wasn't waiting for a blessing.
He was waiting for the bad news.

Then King David asks, "Is there anyone left from that house that I can show the kindness of God to?"

They carry Mephibosheth in. He hits the floor. He's sure this is the end.

And the King says his name.
Then: "Fear not."
Then: "You'll eat at my table. Every day."

And Mephibosheth says, "Why would you look at a dead dog like me?"

The King is talking table. He's talking dog.

Write these down:

You can get a man out of Lo-debar in a day. Getting Lo-debar out of the man takes grace.

God didn't fix his feet before He gave him a seat.

And at the King's table, the tablecloth covers the limp.

So today:
Stop flinching at the knock. It's not a sword. It's an invitation.
Stop introducing yourself by your worst chapter.
Pull up the chair.

Say it out loud:
"Fear not — I shall eat bread at my King's table continually."

No line. No doors at 6. No limited stock.

Sit down.
Your seat was set before you ever started running.

In Jesus' name.
