=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
Go, and Do Thou Likewise

A lawyer asked Jesus a trick question.

Not an honest one. A lawyer question — built to shrink a command down to something manageable.

"Who is my neighbor?"

Translation: how small can I legally make this list?

Jesus didn't answer with a definition. He answered with a dying man on the side of a road.

A priest walked by him. A Levite walked by him. Two men whose entire job was holiness — and both did math instead of mercy.

Then an enemy showed up. The one group this crowd had already decided couldn't possibly love God right. And he was the only one who stopped.

He didn't just feel bad for the guy. What he actually spent on a stranger who'd have crossed the street to avoid him on any other day…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
It's Saturday.

You've got a list today — errands, a kid's game, groceries, maybe a shift you picked up for extra cash.

You are moving. You do not have room today for anyone else's crisis.

Somebody texted and you left it on read.

Somebody at the gas station looked like they needed more than directions, and you looked at your phone instead.

Somebody in your own family has been quietly struggling, and you've decided that's "their process," not your assignment.

You've got your reasons. They're good reasons. You're tired, you're busy, you're not a professional, it's not your lane.

And a quiet lie is riding along with all of it: compassion is for people who have more time than you do.

I want to take you to a road between Jerusalem and Jericho.

A man's been robbed, beaten, and left half-dead in the dirt. A priest walks by — busy with holy things. A Levite walks by — busy with holy things too. Two men who prayed for a living stepped around a dying man like he was debris.

Then a Samaritan shows up. A man this crowd had already decided was too far outside the faith to teach anybody anything about God.

He stops. He kneels in the dust next to a stranger who'd have crossed the street to avoid him on a normal day. He pours out his own oil, his own wine, puts the man on his own ride, and walks the rest of the way himself.

Then he pays the innkeeper and leaves a blank check for whatever it costs after he's gone.

Here's what that road is actually teaching you:

Mercy that costs you nothing was never mercy — it was just a nice feeling with good PR.

Your "I don't have time" and your "that's not my lane" aren't evidence you're busy. They're evidence you've already decided who counts.

You don't find your neighbor by drawing a smaller circle. You find him by stepping outside the one you're already standing in.

So here's what I need you to do today.

Stop managing your compassion like a budget you're trying to stay under.

Pick one person on your list you've been walking past, and actually stop.

Spend something on them you can't get back — time, money, an actual conversation.

Say this out loud right now:

"Lord, make me love in deed and in truth today, not just in word."

Stop stepping around the people God keeps putting right in your path.

Go, and do thou likewise — and heaven will cover whatever it costs you.

In Jesus' name.
