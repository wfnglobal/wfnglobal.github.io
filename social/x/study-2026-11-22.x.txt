=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
Both Sons Were Lost

We call it the parable of the prodigal son, like the story ends when he gets his robe and his ring.

Jesus kept talking.

There's a second son in this parable. Never left home. Never touched a far country. Worked the field every day like he was told.

And when he hears a party going on for his brother, he won't even go inside.

His own father has to walk out and beg him.

Listen to what he actually says: "Lo, these many years do I serve thee." Serve. Not "live with you." Not "be your son."

A man can live under his father's roof his whole life and still be working it like a job…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
Some of you have been the responsible one in your family for so long nobody even thanks you for it anymore. They just expect it.

You showed up. Every time. You didn't run off, didn't blow the savings, didn't embarrass anybody at Christmas.

You did the chores, kept the job, paid the bill that wasn't technically yours to pay.

And somewhere in there, a quiet resentment moved in and started paying rent. You keep a mental list of everyone who got grace they didn't earn — the sibling, the coworker, the friend who always lands on their feet.

Nobody sees the list. You barely admit it to yourself. But it's there, and it's heavy, and it's turning "faithful" into something that tastes a lot like bitter.

I want to take you to a house with two sons.

One of them took his inheritance early, wasted it in a far country, and came crawling home smelling like the pigs he'd been feeding. His father saw him from a distance, hiked up his own robe, and ran to him. Ran. Best robe, a ring, a fattened calf, a party that night.

The other son never left. He was out in the field working, same as always, when he heard the music. He asked a servant what was going on instead of walking in and asking his own father. And when he found out, he got furious and refused to go inside his own house.

Read his complaint closely: "These many years do I serve thee... thou never gavest me a kid, that I might make merry with my friends." Serve. Like an employee reviewing his contract.

He'd been living in his father's house the whole time, next door to everything he could ever want, and never once actually enjoyed it. Too busy keeping score.

Here's what that house is actually teaching you:

You can break every rule in a far country, or keep every rule in the front yard — both are just two different ways of missing the relationship.

A ledger is not the same thing as a relationship. You can serve someone faithfully for years and still never once let them love you.

The resentment you're carrying about somebody else's grace is usually proof you forgot you already own the house.

So here's what I need you to do today:

Put the ledger down. Stop auditing what everyone else got that you think they didn't earn.

Walk inside the party you've been standing outside of out of pure spite.

Say this out loud:

"Father, I am not Your hired hand. I am Your child, and everything You have is already mine."

Stop serving from the yard like a stranger. Go in and sit down like a son.

The music has been for you this whole time.

In Jesus' name.
