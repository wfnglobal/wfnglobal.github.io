=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
She Loved Much

Simon invited Jesus to dinner.

No water for His feet. No kiss at the door. No oil for His head. The basic manners of that whole culture — skipped, all three.

Then a woman nobody invited walked in. Luke doesn't soften it: "a woman in the city, which was a sinner." The whole town already had a file on her.

She stood behind Him weeping. Washed His feet with her own tears. Let her hair down in public — a scandal on its own — and used it as a towel. Then broke open a box of perfume worth more than everything on Simon's table.

Simon watched and thought to himself: if this man were really a prophet, he'd know exactly what kind of woman is touching him right now.

Jesus did know. Better than Simon did.

And He was about to tell the whole room exactly why she was the only person at that table who'd actually loved Him…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
Seven days left in November.

Some of you have had a good year, honestly. You showed up. You didn't cause any scenes. You kept your side of the street clean.

And somewhere in there, church quietly turned into good manners.

You RSVP every Sunday. You say the right things at the right volume. You've never once embarrassed yourself in front of God.

But if you're honest, it's starting to feel less like a relationship and more like a seating arrangement. God's got a chair. You're in it. Nobody's actually touched anybody in years.

That's the lie that sneaks in behind good behavior: that showing up is the same thing as loving Him. That staying out of trouble is basically what devotion looks like.

I want to take you to a dinner party you weren't invited to.

A Pharisee named Simon had Jesus over. Did everything a good host was supposed to do — except the three things that actually mattered. No water for the dust on His feet. No kiss at the door. No oil for His head. Correct seating, zero contact.

Then a woman the whole town had a name for let herself in anyway. Reputation already ruined, nothing left to protect. She fell apart at His feet — tears doing the washing, her own hair doing the drying, a box of perfume she probably couldn't afford cracked open and poured out.

Simon sat there offended. She sat there undone.

And Jesus looked at the man with perfect manners and said: you gave Me a seat. She gave Me everything she had left. Her sins, which are many, are forgiven — for she loved much. But the one who thinks he was forgiven little loves little.

Here's what that dinner table is actually teaching you:

A seat at the table is not the same thing as touching Him.

You don't love your way into forgiveness. You love big because you finally saw how big the bill was.

The people who think they don't have much to be forgiven for are usually the ones loving God the least — not because they're worse, but because they never let it cost them anything.

So here's what I need you to do today.

Stop hosting Jesus from a polite distance. Let Him close enough to actually touch — your schedule, your money, your pride, the parts of your story you'd rather keep off the guest list.

Bring Him whatever's left in your own alabaster box. Don't wait until you're respectable enough to pour it out.

Say this out loud:

"My sins, which are many, are forgiven — so today I will love much."

Stop managing Him like a dinner guest. Fall apart at His feet like someone who actually knows what she was forgiven for.

The posture that silences a whole room isn't good manners. It's a love that finally understood the debt.

In Jesus' name.
