=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
Buy Your Own Oil

Ten bridesmaids. Ten lamps. One wedding.

Five packed extra oil. Five didn't bother.

Then the bridegroom took his time. Hours turned into midnight. And here's the part nobody tells you — all ten of them fell asleep. Not just the careless five. Everybody.

The wise ones weren't more spiritual that night. They were more prepared weeks before it.

At midnight somebody shouted: "Behold, the bridegroom cometh!"

Ten women jumped up. Ten lamps needed trimming. And five of them discovered, in that exact second, that they had nothing left to burn.

They asked to borrow. The wise five said no — and it wasn't cruelty. It was the truest thing said all night…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
It's Tuesday. Thanksgiving is two days away.

Half the country is out buying turkeys, flights, and last-minute everything.

There's one thing on nobody's shopping list this week: a little more oil for their own lamp.

Some of you have been running on fumes for a while now.

Still showing up. Still saying the prayers. Still holding the lamp up so nobody notices anything's wrong.

But if you're honest, you've been coasting on something you didn't personally refill in a long time — your mother's intercession, a sermon from three years ago, a season when you actually used to seek God instead of just mentioning Him.

The quiet lie that comes with coasting is simple: "I'll fill back up later, when things slow down."

Later is a lie. Midnight doesn't check your calendar.

I want to take you to a wedding that never actually gets to happen in this story — because it gets interrupted first.

Ten bridesmaids. Ten lamps. Five packed extra oil before they ever left the house. Five didn't bother — their lamps looked exactly the same in the daylight, so why would it matter.

The bridegroom took forever. All ten got tired. All ten fell asleep. This was never a story about five holy women and five lazy ones — it's a story about ten ordinary people, and sleep caught every single one of them the same.

Then, at midnight — the worst possible hour, nothing open, nothing fixable fast — somebody shouted: "Behold, the bridegroom cometh!"

Ten women scrambled. Five lamps roared back to life. Five sputtered and went dark, because a wick can look full right up until the second you actually need the fire.

They begged the other five for a splash of oil. And the wise five said something that sounds harsh and was actually the realest thing said that whole night: "Not so — go buy your own."

That's not selfishness. That's a law. Nobody can hand you their walk with God at the door.

Here's what I need you to take from this and never put down again:

A lamp that's lit at six o'clock proves nothing about midnight.

Borrowed oil was never on the market. Not then. Not now.

The door doesn't wait for you to finish the errand you should've run weeks ago.

So here's what you're doing today, not next month:

Go buy your own oil. Pray your own prayer, not just the one said over you. Open your own Bible, not just the verse somebody texted you. Build a walk with God that's actually yours, not one you've been quietly renting from somebody who did the work.

Say this out loud right now:

"I will not be caught at midnight with an empty lamp. I am buying my own oil, starting today."

Fill it now, while it's quiet and nobody's clapping. Keep your wick trimmed, your flask full, your lamp ready.

Because when that cry finally comes, you won't be out running an errand.

You'll already be inside.

In Jesus' name.
