=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
Doest Thou Well to Be Angry?

Jonah preached the shortest sermon in the Bible — eight words, zero enthusiasm.

The whole city repented anyway.

Biggest revival in the entire Old Testament. Bigger than anything Elijah ever saw.

And Jonah was furious about it.

Not scared. Not sad. Furious — at his own success.

He finally told God the real reason he ran from this assignment back in chapter one. It wasn't fear of Nineveh.

It was fear that God would actually forgive them.

"I knew that thou art a gracious God, merciful, slow to anger…" — Jonah, quoting God's own character back at Him like it was a personal betrayal.

So God grew him a plant for shade. Jonah loved that plant more than he loved a hundred and twenty thousand souls…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
It's a little past seven in the morning, and wherever you're reading this, the sun is already doing to your day what it once did to a man on a hillside outside Nineveh: turning up the heat on an anger he never actually dealt with.

Some of you did everything right.

You obeyed when it hurt. You forgave when it cost you something real. You stayed faithful in a dark room nobody else ever saw you in.

And now you're watching somebody who did none of that get the apology, the breakthrough, the fresh start — the mercy — you've been waiting years for.

The quiet lie that shows up right there is simple: if they get let off that easy, mine must not have meant anything.

I want to take you to that hillside, because a prophet named Jonah sat in that exact feeling until it nearly killed him.

God sent him to preach to a city he despised. He preached eight unenthusiastic words. The whole city repented — the biggest revival in the entire Old Testament.

And Jonah was furious. Not because it failed. Because it worked.

He told God straight to His face: I ran the first time because I knew You'd be merciful, and I didn't want You to be. So God grew him a plant for shade, let a worm kill it overnight, and asked him a question that's still waiting on an answer two thousand years later: doest thou well to be angry?

Here's what that hillside will teach you if you let it:

Mercy toward you always feels like relief. Mercy toward your enemy always feels like injustice — until you remember you were somebody's enemy too.

You can have better theology than everyone in the room and still carry a heart too small for your own doctrine.

God never asked Jonah to feel differently about it. He just asked him to come down off the hill.

So come down. Stop auditing who deserves what. Let the plant die if it has to die — it was never the actual point.

Say this out loud:

"I knew You were gracious and merciful and slow to anger — and today I choose to actually want that for the one I've been keeping score against."

Forgive the one mercy already covered. Watch what it does to the weight you've been carrying instead of them.

In Jesus' name.
