=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
Are Not Abana and Pharpar Better?

Naaman had everything.

Rank. Respect. A king who owed him his throne.

But he was a leper.

A servant girl — his own captive — told him there was a prophet in Israel who could heal him.

So he showed up with horses, a chariot, and a small fortune in silver and gold.

Expected Elisha to come out personally. Wave a hand over the wound. Call down something dramatic.

Instead a servant opened the door and said six words: go wash in the Jordan, seven times.

Naaman was furious.

"Aren't the rivers back home better than anything Israel has?"

He nearly let his pride talk him out of the one instruction standing between him and his own healing…

=== C) SHARE POST (reply under your poster post, or post it on its own) ===
Your pride will keep you sick arguing about which river is nicer.

God didn't ask Naaman for a better river. He asked for plain obedience.

"If the prophet had bid thee do some great thing, wouldest thou not have done it? how much rather then, when he saith to thee, Wash, and be clean?" — 2 Kings 5:13

Pray: Lord, stop me from needing to be impressed by my own obedience before I'll give it.

Send this to somebody holding out for a harder assignment than the simple one God already gave them.

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
It's November 27. Thirty-four days left in 2026.

Some of you have spent most of those days waiting for a sign big enough to match how hard this year has been.

A word. A breakthrough. Something dramatic enough to prove God noticed.

You've prayed the prayer. You've named the mountain. You've told God exactly how impressive the miracle should look when it finally shows up.

And underneath all that waiting is a quiet suspicion: maybe the answer never came because you weren't given anything big enough to call faith.

I want to take you to a river in Israel, because a famous general had that exact same suspicion.

Naaman had rank, money, and a king who owed him his throne. He also had leprosy, and no amount of rank was fixing that.

He showed up at the prophet's house with a war chest and a chariot, expecting a show. Instead a servant opened the door and said: go wash in the Jordan, seven times.

Naaman was insulted. He had rivers back home that were clearer, prettier, closer. Why would heaven send a great man to muddy water for an ordinary errand?

He almost walked away from his own miracle over a technicality.

Here's what that river will teach you, if your pride lets it:

Pride doesn't usually reject God. It just negotiates the terms.

The instruction was never too small for the miracle. You were too big for the instruction.

God isn't auditioning for your approval. He's just asking you to go down.

So stop ranking God's plain instruction against what you think your situation deserves.

Stop waiting for an assignment impressive enough to be worth your obedience.

Go do the small thing. Make the call. Say the apology. Forgive the name you've been avoiding. Read the chapter you've been skipping. Dip the seventh time even when the first six changed nothing you could see.

Say this out loud:

"Lord, I will not despise the day of small obedience. Wash me, and I will be clean."

Go down. Dip as many times as you're told.

The river was never the miracle — the obedience was, and it's still open to you with thirty-four days left to use it.

In Jesus' name.
