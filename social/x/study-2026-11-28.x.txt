=== B) LONG POST (X Premium long-form; post ~6–7 PM) ===
Why Sit We Here Until We Die?

Samaria was starving so badly a donkey's head sold for eighty pieces of silver.

Four lepers sat outside the gate — too sick to live inside the walls, too weak to survive outside them.

So they did the math out loud.

Stay here, we die. Go into the city, the famine kills us there too. Fall into the enemy's camp — worst case, we just die anyway, same as the plan we already had.

At twilight they got up and walked toward the army that had been starving them.

They expected swords.

What they found instead was tents standing wide open, horses still tied, silver and gold just sitting there — and not one soldier anywhere in sight…

=== D) STORY POST (X Premium long-form; standalone; post ~9–10 AM Toronto, ahead of the lunchtime poster) ===
It's Saturday morning, and for once nobody's expecting anything from you.

Some of you have spent this whole week running numbers on something you're scared to admit out loud.

The job. The marriage. The body that isn't healing the way you prayed. The dream that's gone so quiet you've started calling it dead instead of sleeping.

You've done the math more than once.

If I stay here, nothing changes. If I move, it might not work either.

So you've landed on the safest-feeling option: don't move. Wait. Survive. Call it patience.

And a lie has been sitting right next to that math the whole time:

Maybe this is just how it's going to be for you now.

I want to take you to a city gate in Samaria, because four men sat exactly where you're sitting, and their situation was worse than yours.

Siege outside. Famine inside so bad a donkey's head sold for eighty pieces of silver. Four lepers, too sick to live inside the walls, too weak to survive outside them, parked permanently at the gate like the city's forgotten freight.

They did the math out loud, the same way you've been doing it in your head: sit here, we die. Go in, the famine kills us there too. So why not fall into the enemy's camp — if they kill us, we just die, which was already the plan.

They got up at twilight with nothing but a bad diagnosis and worse odds.

And the camp they walked into, expecting swords, was already empty. God ran off an entire army with a sound effect before these four men ever got there.

Write these down:

When every option ends the same way, moving stops being risky and starts being free.

You don't need faith like a mountain to get up. Sometimes you just need math that finally runs out of excuses.

The miracle doesn't care how unimpressive your reason for moving was. It still counts.

Here's your charge today.

Stop calling your stillness patience. Get up at whatever twilight you're in right now. Walk toward the thing you've been most afraid of, because staying put was never actually safer — you just liked pretending it was.

Say this out loud:

"Lord, I will not sit here until I die. I'm getting up, and I'm trusting You for whatever's on the other side of this gate."

Get up. Walk toward it.

The camp that's been terrifying you has probably been empty for longer than you think.

In Jesus' name.
